```
Nothing on this plain is temporary. Every step two surveyors take is still
lying there, and something reads it.

Two surveyors work a grass plain on an Earth-like world, filling a sample
case. The grass they crush does not stand back up, and by the time they turn
for home their own trail has become a road.

THE SCIENCE
• Earth grasses survive trampling and grazing because their growth tissue —
  the intercalary meristem — sits at the BASE of the leaf, not the tip. Crush
  the top and the leaf keeps growing from underneath. This planet's grass is
  built on the absence of that one tissue.
• Repeated trampling compacts soil and suppresses regrowth. It is why cattle
  tracks and game trails stay readable on Earth grassland for years.
• Large grazing animals reuse established trails instead of breaking new
  ground. The herd behaviour in this film is ordinary; the plain is not.
```
