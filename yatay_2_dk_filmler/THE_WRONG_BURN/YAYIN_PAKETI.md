Burn toward him, and you drift farther away.
Rookie astronaut Sela burns half her reaction control fuel accelerating toward her drifting EVA partner, only to watch him sink below the horizon. With only two bursts remaining, she must trust his voice over her own eyes: to reach him, she has to turn her back and brake.
A two-minute hard sci-fi short film about orbital mechanics, panic, and blind trust in the vacuum of space.

THE SCIENCE
• Gemini IV (1965): Jim McDivitt attempted to rendezvous with his spent Titan II upper stage by burning directly toward it; the thruster burns raised his altitude, slowed his orbital speed, and caused the target to drop away below him (NASA Gemini Program Mission Report).
• Counterintuitive orbital mechanics: Raising orbital altitude increases orbital period, causing an astronaut to lag behind relative to lower-altitude objects.
• Hohmann transfer logic: Closing distance with an object ahead in orbit requires a retrograde burn to drop into a lower, faster orbit before returning.
