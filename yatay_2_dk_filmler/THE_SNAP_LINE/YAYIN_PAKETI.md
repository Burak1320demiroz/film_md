```text
This short film explores why deep-sea saturation divers face lethal pressure changes and why emergency bailout procedures require dropping to the seabed.

THE SCIENCE
• Absolute pressure at 310 meters depth exceeds 32 bar, saturating bodily tissues with inert helium gas (NOAA Diving Manual, 6th Ed.).
• Rapid uncontrolled ascent from 30 atmospheres produces massive gas bubble expansion in the bloodstream, resulting in fatal barotrauma and arterial gas embolism within seconds (U.S. Navy Diving Manual, Rev. 7).
• Commercial saturation divers carry dual high-pressure bailout cylinders providing approximately 8 to 12 minutes of emergency gas at 300 meters depth (IMCA International Code of Practice for Offshore Diving).
```

## 5. PINNED COMMENT

```text
At 310 meters below the surface, the ocean exerts 32 kilograms of pressure on every square centimeter of the human body. Because saturation divers breathe helium-oxygen mixtures for weeks at ambient pressure, their bloodstream is supersaturated with dissolved gas. If pulled up rapidly, those gases instantly boil out of solution like opening a shaken soda can, causing fatal arterial embolism. In offshore commercial diving, when a winch or bell hoist fouls, cutting the umbilical and falling back to the seabed on a 10-minute bailout tank is literally the only medical chance of survival. Would you have the nerve to cut your own air hose in the pitch-black void?
```
