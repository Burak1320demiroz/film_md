### DESCRIPTION (§2) — film-specific lines only

```
She can drive a rod into this dust. One pace further, the same rod bends.

A surveyor is closing a measurement line across an empty plain when she
reaches ground that will not take a marker. The line has to cross it. The
ground was loose the last time she walked through here.

THE SCIENCE
• Fine cohesive regolith compacts permanently under pressure — the Apollo
  boot prints are the small-scale version of this, and they are still there.
• Low gravity reduces weight but not mass and not inertia: at 0.38g a mallet
  stroke costs the same effort it costs on Earth, and a pressurised glove
  resists closing on its own.
• At 0.38g, pressing a given depth into that dust takes roughly 2.6 times
  the mass it would take on Earth. Nothing in this film says what has it.

WATCH NEXT
⬜ one film from the same thin-atmosphere family — title + link
```
