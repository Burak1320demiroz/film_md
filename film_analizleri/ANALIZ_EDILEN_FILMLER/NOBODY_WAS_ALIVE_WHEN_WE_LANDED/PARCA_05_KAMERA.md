## STEP 3 — CAMERA LANGUAGE PATTERN

- Observation vs. Intrusion: The segment begins with an intrusive, unstable camera operating close to the characters on Earth (Shots 1–4, handheld long lenses with heavy rain occlusion). Once the shuttle departs (Shot 6), the camera pulls back into an objective, geometric observer, utilizing wide static frames and smooth tracking shots to emphasize technological scale over human presence.
- POV & Coverage Structure: Dialogue coverage in Scene 1 (Shots 1–4) favors an over-the-shoulder split that aligns the viewer with Captain's confrontational perspective. During orbital transit (Shots 10–19), coverage shifts to ensemble tracking and circular window framing, framing all three crew members as shared captives of the vehicle. In the briefing room (Shots 20–27), coverage isolates characters into single-person setups around the holographic light source.
- Recurring Setups:
  - Circular Window Frames: Used in Shot 14 and Shot 19 to encase character heads within round geometric apertures against outer space.
  - Holographic Key Light: Returned to in Shots 21, 22, 24, and 26, using the glowing table as the sole illuminating source in an otherwise pitch-black room.
- Scale Strategy: The segment moves from cramped medium close-ups (Shots 1–4), expands into massive extreme wide shots during orbital departure (Shots 7, 8, 10, 16), and then steadily contracts back down into intimate, tight close-ups (Shots 22, 24, 27) as the long duration and isolation of the mission are laid out.

## STEP 4 — THE MOST AUTHORED DECISION

Shot 23 (03:07–03:16): Overhead CU of the Holographic Globe

The Choice: Instead of staying in standard mid-shot dialogue coverage around the table, the director cuts directly to an absolute overhead (bird's-eye) static shot of the holographic table, capturing the spinning blue surface of HD 2181 C in complete isolation against pitch blackness. Only the cropped hands of the crew are visible around the glowing perimeter.

What it accomplishes: This setup completely removes character emotion from the frame, positioning the unmapped planet as a giant, looming void that visually swallows the crew. It reinforces the dialogue ("94% of that landmass has never had a human being standing on it") by showing the vastness of the target object relative to the minute, disembodied hands surrounding it.
