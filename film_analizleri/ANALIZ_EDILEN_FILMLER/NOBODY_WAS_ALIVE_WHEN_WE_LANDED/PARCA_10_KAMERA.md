## STEP 3 — CAMERA LANGUAGE PATTERN

- What does the camera DO here? It observes with cold detachment. Rather than dynamically chasing action, it waits for characters to move through static frames or pushes in imperceptibly to underline stillness and isolation.
- Whose point of view does coverage favour? Coverage strongly favors the female lead (Shots 3, 6, 13, 17) through tighter, highly textured framing, before completely abandoning subjective perspective for an omniscient, objective point of view during the exterior and ship sequences (Shots 14–22).
- Recurring setups: Profile two-shots anchored by glowing screens (Shots 7, 8) and low-angle, one-point perspective wide shots of the cryo corridor (Shots 16, 18, 20).
- Scale strategy: The segment deliberately moves from intimate and conversational (ECU/MCU in Shots 1–12) to extremely expansive and stark (WS/EWS in Shots 14–20). This expanding scale tracks the narrative shift from human deliberation and conflict to the vast, unavoidable reality of the spacecraft's journey.

## STEP 4 — THE MOST AUTHORED DECISION

Shots 7 and 8. Standard coverage of this dialogue would rely on over-the-shoulder singles to build emotional intimacy or direct conflict. Instead, the director opts for rigid, perfectly profiled two-shots (first wide, then medium). This flattens both characters against the massive glowing data wall behind them, trapping them in the objective reality of the hologram's information. It visually highlights their physical and emotional distance, forcing the audience to observe the space between them rather than inhabit their subjective perspectives.
