## STEP 3 — CAMERA LANGUAGE PATTERN

- Camera Function: The camera primarily chases and leads the protagonist through the jungle, acting as a physical tether to her exertion. Once she reaches the compound, the camera switches to observing her, becoming static and distant to emphasize her isolation.
- Point of View: Coverage heavily favors the subjective experience of the protagonist (Reese). She is anchored in the center frame for almost every tracking shot (Shots 7, 10, 13, 19). The segment briefly shifts to an objective/environmental threat perspective (Shot 11 holding on the empty path, Shot 15 on the alien) to build dramatic irony before returning to her.
- Recurring setups: The segment repeatedly utilizes a "leading steadicam" setup where the subject runs directly toward the retreating lens (Shots 7, 10, 12, 19). It also creates a distinct visual rhyme between her struggle to open the door outside (Shot 23, MCU eye-level) and close it inside (Shot 25, MCU eye-level).
- Scale strategy: The segment moves in a U-shape. It begins with immense, indifferent EWS establishing shots (Shots 1-4). It pushes into tight MCU/CU scales during the chase to trap the viewer in her physical panic (Shots 8-12). Finally, it pushes back out to WS/EWS scales upon reaching the compound (Shots 17, 27) to re-emphasize her absolute smallness and vulnerability.

## STEP 4 — THE MOST AUTHORED DECISION

Shot 24 (03:12–03:18): Instead of following the protagonist through the door from the outside, the camera abruptly cuts to the pitch-black interior of the facility before she enters. It holds static as the massive door slowly opens, revealing her small, brightly silhouetted form against the exterior daylight. This choice subverts the expectation of sanctuary; by planting the viewer's POV in the dark void waiting for her, the camera argues that this structure is not a safe haven, but rather a tomb she is sealing herself inside.
