## STEP 3 — CAMERA LANGUAGE PATTERN

- What the camera does: It actively alternates between observing (via clinical sniper scopes and high-altitude drone perspectives) and intruding (via claustrophobic, mud-flecked handheld close-ups inside human helmets).
- Point of view coverage: Coverage heavily favors an embedded, subjective human perspective (the soldiers in the dirt), but periodically punctures it with objective, god-like surveillance shots (drone flights, high-angle tactical overviews).
- Recurring setups: Returns repeatedly to extreme close-ups of helmet visors reflecting external lights or screens (Shots 3, 8, 10), serving as an emotional anchor point for the characters' internal panic.
- Scale strategy: The segment moves from controlled, tight framing (scopes and faceplates) out to a massive, sprawling macro scale (the drone shots of the quarry), then collapses back down into frantic ground-level tracking shots and an intimate close-up inside the truck bed. This tracks the narrative expansion from a hidden recon mission into total exposure and chaotic retreat.

## STEP 4 — THE MOST AUTHORED DECISION

The segment's most deliberate camera decision occurs in Shot 6, where the camera cuts from a grounded, handheld view of a small drone taking off directly into a smooth, sweeping drone-eye view (WS) soaring over the giant industrial crater. Instead of keeping the audience trapped in subjective human perspective, the director uses a diegetic device (the military reconnaissance drone) to earn a massive, objective shift in scale.
