## STEP 3 — CAMERA LANGUAGE PATTERN

- What the camera does: The camera acts as a detached, cinematic observer. It rarely uses handheld agitation, choosing instead smooth tracking dollies or completely static, architectural compositions.
- POV & Coverage: Coverage leans toward the recruits (Tigs, Deck, Sato) rather than the recruiters. In each recruitment mini-chapter (Gym, Bar, Workshop), the recruiters are initially obscured or framed in high-contrast silhouette (Shots 6, 13), forcing the lens to favor the emotional realization on the recruits' faces (Shots 8, 12, 23).
- Recurring setups:
  - The Illuminated Face: Tight CU/MCU shots where the character's face is lit primarily by an emissive device/tablet in darkness (Shot 8, Shot 23).
  - Threshold Framing: Framing subjects through architectural openings, doorways, or windows (Shot 4 down stairs, Shot 13 in doorway, Shot 18 through window, Shot 19 under archway).
- Scale strategy: Each character introduction begins at a wide/medium scale establishing environment (Gym WS, Bar MWS, Workshop WS), moves into tight CUs for the pitch/reinstatement, and expands back out to an EWS/WS scale once assembled at the hangar.

## STEP 4 — THE MOST AUTHORED DECISION

Shot 18 (02:00–02:07) — Framing through a rain-slicked windowpane.

The Choice: Rather than placing the camera outside on the street with the two characters during a critical dialogue beat about past trauma, the director places the camera inside a building looking out through a rain-covered glass window, capturing the reflection of interior pub patrons overlaid onto Tigs and Deck.

What it accomplishes: It visually reinforces the isolation of the two veterans from normal civilian society. The audience is physically distanced from their conversation, viewing them as subjects trapped inside a rainy glass frame, emphasizing that their conversation belongs to a separate world.
