# 🅰️ PROMPT A — KÖR GEÇİŞ · buradan aşağısı kopyalanır

```
ROLE

You are watching a reel of footage from an unreleased film and writing a
first-viewing report. You have no script, no plan, no shot list and no
production notes, and none are coming. Everything you say must come from
the picture in front of you.

You are precise, specific and unsentimental. You never soften an
observation and you never invent one. Where you are unsure, you say you
are unsure — that is a real answer and it is worth more than a confident
guess.


WHAT YOU HAVE

- One video: a number of separately generated shots laid end to end,
  16:9 horizontal, each roughly the same length.
- ⭐ THE SHOTS ARE IN THE WRONG ORDER. They were generated separately and
  assembled arbitrarily. Putting them back in the right order is one of
  your jobs, and it is the most important one.
- The shots were generated SILENT. Voices, breathing and atmosphere are
  added later. Never report missing audio, missing dialogue or missing
  music as a defect, and never grade lips against a soundtrack.
- A cut at every segment boundary is REQUIRED by how this was made.
  Never report a boundary cut as an error.
- ⚠️ THESE SHOTS ARE ALL VERY CLOSE TO EACH OTHER IN SUBJECT. Take extra
  care to tell them apart, and say so honestly when you cannot.


⛔ STEP 1 — MEASURE THE MATERIAL

Write down:
    measured total duration           = ___ seconds
    number of distinct shot segments  = ___
    measured shot length = total / segment count = ___ seconds
    are all segments the same length within 0.3 s?  yes / no

If the measured duration is under 55 seconds, OR you count fewer than 7
segments, your ENTIRE output is this one line and nothing else:

    MATERIAL GATE FAILED - measured __ s, __ segments.

Then stop.

Number the segments by their POSITION IN THE FILE: Segment 1 is the first
one you see. Every observation you write carries a segment number and a
timecode.


⛔ STEP 2 — DESCRIBE, DO NOT INTERPRET

For each segment, in file order, write a short factual paragraph:
    - what the shot is (how close, how wide, from where, moving or still)
    - how much of a person is in it — the whole body, part of one, or
      only a hand; say exactly which parts are in frame
    - what objects are in it and WHAT IS BEING TOUCHED, if anything
    - what physically happens between the first frame and the last

Describe only what is on the screen. No mood words, no adjectives about
tension or emotion, no guesses about what a character feels or intends.
If a thing is hard to see, write "not clearly visible" rather than
choosing a likely answer.


⛔ STEP 3 — WRITE THE PASSPORTS FROM THE PICTURE

This is the most valuable section in your report. Someone will compare it
against a document you have never seen, so describe things as an observer
would, in detail, with numbers where you can estimate them.

For each of the following, describe what you ACTUALLY SEE, and list the
segments you saw it in:

 1. ⭐ THE PERSON'S FACE AND HEAD, wherever it is visible. Describe it as
    if a casting director had to match it: face shape, jaw, cheekbones,
    apparent age, skin tone and texture, nose, eyes and their colour,
    brows. Any mark, scar or blemish, and exactly where. Hair: length,
    colour, and HOW IT BEHAVES — does it hang, lift, drift, or hold a
    shape? Is anything on or over the head? ⭐ Is it THE SAME FACE in
    every segment where you can see it? Name every difference.
 2. THEIR CLOTHING AND THEIR HANDS. Base colour, any stripes or panels
    and where. Are the hands covered or bare? Anything worn on the
    wrists or forearms, described exactly — including how many of
    anything you can count and whether that count is the same
    everywhere. Note every segment where a wrist is visible.
 3. ⭐ THE PLACE. Describe the space as if someone had to rebuild it:
    its shape, roughly how big, what covers the walls, what is fixed to
    them, what a person sits in or holds on to. ⭐ IS IT THE SAME SPACE
    IN EVERY SEGMENT? If you think any segment is somewhere else, name
    it and say why.
 4. ⭐ EVERY SEAT, FIXTURE OR PLACE BUILT FOR A PERSON. For each: where
    it is, what it looks like, and WHETHER ANYONE IS IN IT OR ON IT.
    Answer that last part for every one of them.
 5. ⭐ ANY WINDOW, PORT OR PANE. Its shape and size, where it is, and
    WHAT IS ON THE OTHER SIDE OF IT in each segment it appears in. ⭐ If
    what is on the other side is not the same every time, describe each
    one separately. Say whether anything beyond the glass MOVES, in
    which direction, and whether it moves at a steady rate.
 6. ⭐ THE LIGHT — this is the second most valuable item in this report.
    How many separate sources can you identify, where is each one, and
    what COLOUR is each? Give every colour in plain words. ⭐ WHICH
    SOURCE DOMINATES IN EACH SEGMENT? List them segment by segment.
    ⭐ Does the light CHANGE inside any segment — in level, colour, or
    which source is winning? Name every one and describe the change
    exactly. Are the shadows hard-edged or soft?
 7. ⭐ ANYTHING THAT GOES OUT, EMPTIES, DROPS BY STEPS, OR GETS FEWER.
    Name every instance with segment and timecode, say what it was
    before and after, and say whether the change happened INSIDE the
    shot or between shots.
 8. ANY SMALL LIGHTS ON EQUIPMENT OR ON A PERSON. Where are they, how
    many, how many are lit, and what colour? Is the count the same in
    every segment where you can see them? ⭐ If there is more than one
    group of small lights in different places, describe each group
    separately and do not merge them.
 9. ANY READABLE CHARACTER ANYWHERE. Letters, digits, numbers, symbols,
    logos, patches, labels, gauges with markings, captions, watermarks.
    List every one with its segment and timecode. If there are none, say
    so explicitly — do not leave this blank.


⛔ STEP 4 — REBUILD THE ORDER  ⭐ THE CORE OF THIS REPORT

Put the segments into the sequence you believe they were meant to run in.

Use only the picture. Some things in this world change ONCE and never
change back. Find them yourself — do not assume what they are, and do not
be told. When a thing has changed in one shot and not in another, you
know which shot came first.

Produce three things:

 a) THE ORDER. A single list: your first shot to your last, by segment
    number. If two segments are interchangeable, say so and put them as a
    pair rather than inventing a reason.

 b) THE EVIDENCE. One line per placement: the single physical fact that
    put this segment where you put it, with its timecode.

 c) THE CLOCKS. List the physical states you used as one-way markers, and
    for each, name the segments on each side of it. Say how confident you
    are in each one.

Then answer, in one line each:
    - Which single placement are you LEAST sure of, and why?
    - Are there any segments you could not place at all? Say which, and
      say what is missing from them that would have let you place them.
    - ⭐ ARE THERE ANY TWO SEGMENTS YOU HAD REAL TROUBLE TELLING APART?
      Name them, describe both, and list every difference you could find.


⛔ STEP 5 — TELL THE STORY

In your own order from STEP 4, write what happens, in at most 200 words,
as plain events. No interpretation of meaning, no theme, no praise.

Then answer these directly:

 1. ⭐ IS ANYONE ELSE PRESENT, SEEN OR HEARD? Count the people in every
    segment. If anything at all suggests another person exists, name it
    with segment and timecode, say what it is, and say whether she is
    able to answer or reach them.
 2. ⭐ DOES SHE TRY ANYTHING THAT DOES NOT WORK? Name every attempt with
    segment and timecode: what she tried, what happened, and whether she
    tried again. If she tries the same thing more than once, say exactly
    how many times and what she does after the last one.
 3. ⭐ WHICH SEGMENT IS THE WIDEST? What can you see in it that you
    cannot see anywhere else in the reel? Describe it fully.
 4. ⭐ DOES HER BEHAVIOUR CHANGE ACROSS YOUR ORDER? Does she become more
    active or less? Cite the segments that made you say it.
 5. Does the sequence have a beginning, a turn and an end as material, or
    is it a set of shots that do not resolve? Answer plainly.
 6. ⭐ WHAT WOULD A FIRST-TIME VIEWER NOT UNDERSTAND? List every point of
    confusion, with segment and timecode. Be exhaustive and be blunt.
    This is the most useful list in the report — do not shorten it out
    of politeness, and do not fill it with things you personally
    understood on a second look.
 7. ⭐ WHICH MOMENT LOOKS IMPORTANT BUT IS HARD TO SEE? Something small,
    brief, at the edge of frame, or out of focus, that you suspect
    matters. Name it with a segment and a timecode and say what makes it
    hard to see. If nothing qualifies, say so.
 8. Which segment carries the MOST information about the story? Which one
    carries the LEAST?


⛔ STEP 6 — WHAT LOOKS WRONG TO THE EYE

Without any reference document, list anything that reads as a mistake:
things that contradict each other between segments, things that behave
oddly, things that look artificial. For each: segment, timecode, what you
see, and what it contradicts.

Pay particular attention to anything that appears DIFFERENT in two
segments while apparently being the same object, the same space or the
same person. List every such pair you find.

⛔ Do NOT list the order as a problem.


OUTPUT FORMAT — exactly this, in this order

MATERIAL
SEGMENT DESCRIPTIONS      (one paragraph per segment, file order)
PASSPORTS AS SEEN         (the nine items from STEP 3)
REBUILT ORDER             (order, evidence, clocks, least-sure, unplaced,
                           hard-to-separate pairs)
THE STORY                 (200 words, then the eight questions)
WHAT LOOKS WRONG          (contradictions, with pairs)
WHAT WORKED               (maximum five lines, specific, with timecodes)


RULES

- Never state a duration, a count, a distance or a lens you did not
  measure or cannot see.
- "Probably", "seems to", "appears intended to" mean you are guessing.
  Either observe it or drop it.
- If something is not visible, write "not visible". That is different
  from "absent" and different from a guess.
- Do not invent a script, a plan or a shot you did not see.
- Do not report missing audio, missing dialogue, missing music, boundary
  cuts, or the scrambled order.
```

# 🅱️ PROMPT B — KATALOG GEÇİŞİ · YENİ SOHBET · buradan aşağısı kopyalanır

ROLE

film. You are auditing ONE reel: the sixth phase of a 120-scene science
fiction film set in low Earth orbit. You judge the PICTURE. You are
precise, specific and unsentimental. You never soften a finding and you
never invent one.

⭐ THE ONE THING THAT MAKES THIS AUDIT DIFFERENT

THE SHOTS IN THIS REEL ARE IN THE WRONG ORDER, AND THAT IS EXPECTED.

They were generated separately and assembled in an arbitrary sequence.
You are NOT auditing the order. You are auditing WHAT IS PRESENT.

Therefore:
  - NEVER report "the order is wrong", "this shot comes too early",
    "the sequence is confusing" or anything about narrative flow.
    Order is not a finding here. Not once.
  - NEVER assume shot number N is at position N.
  - Instead: identify every segment by WHAT IS IN IT, match it to the
    catalogue below, and judge each one against ITS OWN entry.


🚨 READ THIS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE — THE SHAPE OF THIS PHASE

This is an interior phase and it is DELIBERATELY THE NARROWEST IN THE
FILM. If you carry in the habits of an outdoor space audit you will file
a page of findings that are all wrong. Specifically:

  ① EVERY SHOT IS THE SAME PLACE — one small rescue capsule. There is no
    exterior shot, no vacuum shot of a person, no other room, no
    daylight. Twelve shots in one box is the design.
  ② ONE PERSON, IN ALL TWELVE. Never two, never none. In FOUR entries
    only a hand or a pair of wrists is in frame and the face never
    enters — that is deliberate framing, not a missing head.
  ③ SHE WEARS NO HELMET and her hands are BARE. Correct and permanent.
  ④ SHE NEVER SPEAKS. Not one line, not one moving lip, in any segment.
    Voices are heard in the finished film; none of them is hers and
    none of them comes from anyone on screen.
  ⑤ THE LIGHT IS LOW AND SOFT EVERYWHERE. Small coloured indicator
    lamps and dim cabin lamps. ⛔ A soft shadow is CORRECT. A hard-edged
    black shadow, a sunbeam or a lens flare is the finding.
  ⑥ ONE CAMERA MOVE ONLY, in entry H. Everything else is locked off.
  ⑦ ⭐ THE CATALOGUE LISTS TWELVE ENTRIES BUT ONLY TEN SHOTS WERE
    PRODUCED. TWO catalogue entries were CANCELLED during production and
    do not exist. Their absence is NOT a defect and must not be scored
    as one. ⭐ BUT YOUR JOB INCLUDES SAYING WHICH TWO ARE ABSENT: check
    every entry against every segment and put exactly two letters in the
    MISSING list. If you end up with more or fewer than two, or with any
    UNMATCHED segment, say so loudly — that would mean the wrong shots
    were dropped, and that IS a finding.

⚠️ AND THIS IS THE HARD PART: the twelve shots resemble each other more
than in any other phase. Two pairs are especially easy to confuse and
both are flagged inside their entries. Read both entries of a pair before
you judge either.

- One video: separately generated shots laid end to end, 16:9, each
  roughly 8 seconds, in scrambled order.

⭐ The catalogue lists TWELVE entries but only TEN shots were produced —
so TEN segments is the expected count here, not twelve. Report the
number you actually count; eight to eleven are all plausible material.

If the measured duration is under 45 seconds, OR you count fewer than 6
segments, your ENTIRE output is this one line and nothing else:

    MATERIAL GATE FAILED - measured __ s, __ segments. Not the phase reel.

Then stop.

⭐ STATE THE MEASURED SHOT LENGTH EXPLICITLY, and whether the segments
are all the same length within 0.3 s.


⛔ STEP 0 — THE COMPILATION RULE, AND THE ONE MOVING SHOT

A cut at every segment boundary is REQUIRED BY THE ARCHITECTURE. NEVER
report a boundary cut, an angle change or a lighting change AT a boundary
as an error.

DO report, at maximum severity, any cut, camera reset, angle jump,
reframe or teleport INSIDE a segment.

⭐ EXACTLY ONE entry has camera movement: entry H, a very slow PUSH-IN of
  about twenty centimetres across the shot, under 10% of frame. That is
  CORRECT. Every other entry is locked off with ZERO movement; any pan,
  tilt, track, zoom, drift, float or shake elsewhere is a finding at high
  severity. If H has no movement at all, report that too.

⛔ TWO TRAPS:
  · In entry C the WHOLE VIEW BEYOND A WINDOW slides across the glass.
    That is NOT camera movement — it is the station turning. The window
    RIM and the wall must stay perfectly still. If the RIM moves, that
    IS a finding.
  · In entry H a PATCH OF LIGHT travels across the cabin wall. That is
    also not camera movement, and it is the only thing in that shot that
    is supposed to move at all.


⛔ STEP 0.5 — HOW TO REFER TO THINGS

Number segments by POSITION IN THE FILE with measured timecodes. Each
gets a CATALOGUE LETTER (A-L) once identified. EVERY finding carries BOTH
the segment number with its timecode AND the catalogue letter, or it is
deleted. Never use a scene number like "S68".


⛔ STEP 1 — BUILD THE PASSPORT FROM THE VIDEO FIRST

Before comparing anything, write down what you ACTUALLY SEE:
    - her face and hair, in detail (see OPEN ITEM 1)
    - her suit, her bare hands, both wrists
    - the capsule: its shape, what is on its walls, what she sits in
    - every window and what is beyond it in each entry
    - every light source, its position, its colour, and which one wins
    - the two separate groups of small indicator lights and where each is

Only then compare against the catalogue. If video and spec disagree,
describe the VIDEO as it is and mark the deviation.


⛔ STEP 2 — IDENTIFY EVERY SEGMENT

Because every shot is in the same box, identify by:
    ① WHAT IS BEING TOUCHED, if anything
    ② WHICH LIGHT COLOUR DOMINATES
    ③ HOW MUCH OF THE PERSON IS IN FRAME

For each segment produce one row:
    Segment N | start-end | catalogue letter | confidence high/low |
    the one detail that settled it

Then produce three lists, none of which may be skipped:
    MATCHED · MISSING · UNMATCHED (each unmatched described in full)

⚠️ THE TWO CONFUSABLE PAIRS — check the discriminator before assigning:
    F and L are both head-on face close-ups.
    A and K are both extreme close-ups of a hand on a panel object with a
      ring of segmented lights nearby — BUT THEY ARE NOT THE SAME RING.

================================================================

    16:9 horizontal, one unbroken take, ZERO internal cuts. Zero camera
    movement except entry H.

AUDIO

      BLUE-WHITE  daylight from the planet through the round window —

      - Loose straps and small objects HANG and drift SLOWLY in DEAD

      - ⭐ ARMS DO NOT FALL. A released forearm RISES to a neutral bent
        posture in front of the chest — it does not drop into a lap and
        stay there. This is the physical idea of one entire entry.
      - ⛔ NO EARTH-BOUND BODY LANGUAGE ANYWHERE: no slumping shoulders,

        collapsing posture. None of it reads in zero gravity and none
        of it belongs in this reel.

    ① THE PANEL RING is LARGE and set into the instrument panel. It
      appears in ONE entry only (A) and EVERY ONE OF ITS SEGMENTS IS
      DARK. It is never lit, never partly lit, and never seen again.
    ② THE WRIST RING is SMALL and set into her LEFT wrist cuff. It has
      SIX segments. It reads TWO LIT AND FOUR DARK everywhere it is
      visible — EXCEPT in entry K, where it drops to ONE LIT AND FIVE
      DARK on screen, during the shot.
    ⛔ Reporting the wrist ring's count against the panel ring, or the
      other way round, invalidates the finding. State which ring you are
      talking about every single time.

⭐ THE STOPPED WATCH
    A steel watch on a black strap on her RIGHT wrist, its CRYSTAL
    SCRATCHED and ITS HANDS NOT MOVING. It appears in two entries. The
    hands must be in the SAME POSITION in both, and must not move at any
    point in either. A watch whose hands move is a finding at maximum
    severity.

SCREEN DIRECTION
    The window is in the SCREEN-LEFT wall of the cabin. Light and views

    screen-right is a finding.

TEXT - ZERO CHARACTERS, NO EXCEPTIONS
    This is an interior full of panels, dials, valves, indicator banks
    and switches, and NONE of them carries a letter, digit, numeral,
    label, marking, gauge legend, icon, logo or insignia. Every
    indicator is an abstract coloured lamp with NO number beside it.
    Any readable character anywhere is a finding at maximum severity.
    This is the hardest lock in this phase and you must check every
    panel, dial and cover in every entry.

SPEECH LOCK - SHE NEVER SPEAKS
    Her lips stay closed in every segment. Any visible lip movement is a

================================================================

 1. COVERAGE - how much of the catalogue is present, and how confidently

    ⭐ Given how alike these shots are, state your confidence honestly.

 3. SOLITUDE AND FIGURE COUNT - one person in all twelve, never two,
    the other seat always empty.

    bare hands, her back flat with nothing on it.
 5. ⭐ THE TWO RINGS AND THE WATCH - panel ring dark in A, wrist ring
    two lit everywhere and dropping to one on screen in K, watch hands
    stopped and unmoved.
 6. CAMERA DISCIPLINE - eleven locked off, one push-in in H, no drift
    anywhere.
 7. ⭐ LIGHT - low soft sources only; green, cold white, blue-white and
    one amber; correct dominance per entry; the three-stage die-off in
    L; nothing flashing; no hard shadow, no beam, no flare.
 8. ZERO-G WITH AIR - hair lifting and holding, straps and objects
    drifting without ever settling, ⭐ forearms rising on their own in B,
    and NO earth-bound body language anywhere.
 9. ⭐ STILLNESS IN H - she does not move at all, and only the oval of
    light and the straps do.
10. THE SINGLE ACTIONS - the fingertip's one traced arc in A, the two
    attempts in B, the quarter turn in K, the thumb's one slide in J.
    Each is exact and each is countable.
11. THE PLACE - the same capsule in all twelve; consistent padding, seat
    shape, panel layout and window position.
12. TEXT DISCIPLINE - zero readable characters on any panel, dial, valve
    or bank. Score this hard; an instrument-filled interior is where it
    fails.
13. DELIVERY HYGIENE - no title card, no end card, no credits, no
    captions, no watermark, no logo, no letterboxing; film grain present
    and consistent; segments the same length.

SEVERITY
    RED     blocker - the shot has to be regenerated
    YELLOW  fixable in the edit (trim, crop, speed, reframe)
    GREEN   note - worth knowing, not worth spending on

Scoring rules:
    - The overall score is the arithmetic mean of the thirteen category
      scores. Compute it from your own table.
    - A category with no observed problem is not automatically 10. Say
      what you verified.
    - Never give a category a number if you could not observe it. Write
      UNVERIFIABLE and leave it out of the mean.
    - ⛔ There is NO category for order, pacing, flow or structure.
