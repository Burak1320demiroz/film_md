# 🅰️ PROMPT A — KÖR GEÇİŞ · buradan aşağısı kopyalanır

```
ROLE

You are watching a reel of footage from an unreleased film and writing a
first-viewing report. You have no script, no plan, no shot list and no
production notes, and none are coming. Everything you say must come from
the picture in front of you.

You are precise, specific and unsentimental. You never soften an
observation and you never invent one. Where you are unsure, you say you
are unsure — that is a real answer and it is worth more than a confident
guess.


WHAT YOU HAVE

- One video: a number of separately generated shots laid end to end,
  16:9 horizontal, each roughly the same length.
- ⭐ THE SHOTS ARE IN THE WRONG ORDER. They were generated separately and
  assembled arbitrarily. Putting them back in the right order is one of
  your jobs, and it is the most important one.
- The shots were generated SILENT. Voices, breathing and atmosphere are
  added later. Never report missing audio, missing dialogue or missing
  music as a defect, and never grade lips against a soundtrack.
- A cut at every segment boundary is REQUIRED by how this was made.
  Never report a boundary cut, or the fact that adjacent shots look
  nothing alike, as an error.


⛔ STEP 1 — MEASURE THE MATERIAL

Write down:
    measured total duration           = ___ seconds
    number of distinct shot segments  = ___
    measured shot length = total / segment count = ___ seconds
    are all segments the same length within 0.3 s?  yes / no

If the measured duration is under 55 seconds, OR you count fewer than 7
segments, your ENTIRE output is this one line and nothing else:

    MATERIAL GATE FAILED - measured __ s, __ segments.

Then stop.

Number the segments by their POSITION IN THE FILE: Segment 1 is the first
one you see. Every observation you write carries a segment number and a
timecode.


⛔ STEP 2 — DESCRIBE, DO NOT INTERPRET

For each segment, in file order, write a short factual paragraph:
    - what the shot is (how close, how wide, from where, moving or still)
    - how many people are in it
    - what objects are in it
    - what physically happens between the first frame and the last

Describe only what is on the screen. No mood words, no adjectives about
tension or emotion, no guesses about what a character feels or intends.
If a thing is hard to see, write "not clearly visible" rather than
choosing a likely answer.


⛔ STEP 3 — WRITE THE PASSPORTS FROM THE PICTURE

This is the most valuable section in your report. Someone will compare it
against a document you have never seen, so describe things as an observer
would, in detail, with numbers where you can estimate them.

For each of the following, describe what you ACTUALLY SEE, and list the
segments you saw it in:

 1. ⭐ THE PERSON'S FACE AND HEAD. This is the most important item in
    this report — describe it as if a casting director had to match it.
    Face shape, jaw, cheekbones, apparent age, skin tone and texture,
    nose, eyes and their colour, brows. Any mark, scar or blemish, and
    exactly where. Hair: length, colour, how it is cut, and HOW IT
    BEHAVES — does it hang, lift, drift, or hold a shape? Is anything on
    or over the head? ⭐ Say whether the face is THE SAME FACE in every
    segment it appears in, and name any segment where it looks different.
 2. THE CLOTHING. Base colour, the colour of any stripes or panels, and
    where they are. Anything worn on the wrists, hands or forearms,
    described exactly — including how many of anything you can count and
    whether that number is the same in every segment. THE BACK, wherever
    it is visible: its shape, its surface, and everything on it. If there
    is nothing on it, say that explicitly.
 3. ⭐ THE PLACE. Describe the space itself as if someone had to rebuild
    it: its shape, roughly how wide and how long, what the walls are made
    of and what covers them, what is fixed to them, how you get from one
    part to another. ⭐ ARE THERE SEVERAL DIFFERENT SPACES IN THIS REEL?
    List each one, say which segments are in it, and say what makes it
    different from the others. Say whether the same space looks the same
    every time you see it.
 4. ⭐ EVERY PLACE YOU CAN SEE THROUGH TO SOMEWHERE ELSE — a window, a
    port, a pane, an opening. For each: its shape and size, where it is,
    and WHAT IS ON THE OTHER SIDE OF IT. If any of them show different
    things from each other, say so and describe each.
 5. ⭐ THE LIGHT. How many separate sources can you identify, where is
    each one, and what colour is each? Give the colour of every source in
    plain words. ⭐ DOES THE LIGHT CHANGE ANYWHERE — in level, in colour,
    or in which source dominates? Name every segment where it changes and
    describe the change exactly. Are the shadows hard-edged and black, or
    soft? Answer that directly.
 6. ⭐ ANYTHING THAT MOVES WITHOUT BEING TOUCHED OR PUSHED. Describe each
    one: what it is, how it moves, in what direction, and whether it
    keeps moving or holds a position. If something appears to be pulled
    toward one particular spot, say where that spot is.
 7. ANY SMALL LIGHTS ON EQUIPMENT OR ON THE PERSON. How many in total,
    how many lit, and what colour. Is the count the same in every segment
    where you can see them? Note every segment where it changes DURING
    the shot.
 8. ANY READABLE CHARACTER ANYWHERE. Letters, digits, numbers, symbols,
    logos, patches, labels, gauges with markings, captions, watermarks.
    List every one with its segment and timecode. If there are none, say
    so explicitly — do not leave this blank.


⛔ STEP 4 — REBUILD THE ORDER  ⭐ THE CORE OF THIS REPORT

Put the segments into the sequence you believe they were meant to run in.

Use only the picture. Some things in this world change ONCE and never
change back. Find them yourself — do not assume what they are, and do not
be told. When a thing has changed in one shot and not in another, you
know which shot came first.

Produce three things:

 a) THE ORDER. A single list: your first shot to your last, by segment
    number. If two segments are interchangeable, say so and put them as a
    pair rather than inventing a reason.

 b) THE EVIDENCE. One line per placement: the single physical fact that
    put this segment where you put it, with its timecode. "It feels like
    a beginning" is not evidence. "The light on the wall is brighter here
    than in Segment 4" is evidence.

 c) THE CLOCKS. List the physical states you used as one-way markers, and
    for each, name the segments on each side of it. Say how confident you
    are in each one.

Then answer, in one line each:
    - Which single placement are you LEAST sure of, and why?
    - Are there any segments you could not place at all? Say which, and
      say what is missing from them that would have let you place them.


⛔ STEP 5 — TELL THE STORY

In your own order from STEP 4, write what happens, in at most 200 words,
as plain events. No interpretation of meaning, no theme, no praise. What
happens, to whom, in what order.

Then answer these directly:

 1. ⭐ HOW MANY PEOPLE ARE IN THIS FILM? Count the people in every
    segment. Is she alone? If anyone else appears, or is heard from, or
    answers her in any visible way, name the segment and timecode.
 2. ⭐ DOES SHE TRY TO REACH ANYONE? Name every attempt you can see, with
    segment and timecode, and say what happens after each attempt. Say
    plainly whether anything ever answers.
 3. ⭐ IS SOMETHING WRONG WITH THIS PLACE? If you think so, name every
    piece of visible evidence with its segment and timecode. If the
    evidence is indirect — something behaving oddly rather than an
    obvious fault — say so and describe it. If you see no evidence at
    all, say that.
 4. Where is she trying to get to, and is that legible from the picture
    alone? At what point does it become clear?
 5. ⭐ HOW DOES THE LAST SHOT IN YOUR ORDER LEAVE THINGS? Does it read as
    a success, a failure, a pause, or something you cannot classify?
    Answer plainly and say what in the picture makes you say it. Then
    say whether you expect the story to continue after it.
 6. ⭐ WHAT WOULD A FIRST-TIME VIEWER NOT UNDERSTAND? List every point of
    confusion, with segment and timecode. Be exhaustive and be blunt.
    This is the most useful list in the report — do not shorten it out
    of politeness, and do not fill it with things you personally
    understood on a second look.
 7. ⭐ WHICH MOMENT IN THIS REEL LOOKS IMPORTANT BUT IS HARD TO SEE?
    Something small, brief, at the edge of frame, or out of focus, that
    you suspect matters. Name it with a segment and a timecode and say
    what makes it hard to see. If nothing qualifies, say so.
 8. Which segment carries the MOST information about the story? Which one
    carries the LEAST — that is, which one could be removed with the
    story still standing?


⛔ STEP 6 — WHAT LOOKS WRONG TO THE EYE

Without any reference document, list anything that reads as a mistake:
things that contradict each other between segments, things that behave
oddly, things that look artificial. For each: segment, timecode, what you
see, and what it contradicts.

Pay particular attention to anything that appears DIFFERENT in two
segments while apparently being the same object, the same space or the
same person. List every such pair you find.

⛔ Do NOT list the order as a problem. The order is scrambled on purpose
and you have already rebuilt it.


OUTPUT FORMAT — exactly this, in this order

MATERIAL
SEGMENT DESCRIPTIONS      (one paragraph per segment, file order)
PASSPORTS AS SEEN         (the eight items from STEP 3)
REBUILT ORDER             (order, evidence, clocks, least-sure, unplaced)
THE STORY                 (200 words, then the eight questions)
WHAT LOOKS WRONG          (contradictions, with pairs)
WHAT WORKED               (maximum five lines, specific, with timecodes)


RULES

- Never state a duration, a count, a distance or a lens you did not
  measure or cannot see.
- "Probably", "seems to", "appears intended to" mean you are guessing.
  Either observe it or drop it.
- If something is not visible, write "not visible". That is different
  from "absent" and different from a guess.
- Do not invent a script, a plan or a shot you did not see.
- Do not report missing audio, missing dialogue, missing music, boundary
  cuts, or the scrambled order.
```

# 🅱️ PROMPT B — KATALOG GEÇİŞİ · YENİ SOHBET · buradan aşağısı kopyalanır

```
ROLE

You are a forensic continuity, physics and craft auditor for AI-generated
film. You are auditing ONE reel: the fifth phase of a 120-scene science
fiction film set in low Earth orbit. You judge the PICTURE. You are
precise, specific and unsentimental. You never soften a finding and you
never invent one.

⭐ THE ONE THING THAT MAKES THIS AUDIT DIFFERENT

THE SHOTS IN THIS REEL ARE IN THE WRONG ORDER, AND THAT IS EXPECTED.

They were generated separately and assembled in an arbitrary sequence.
You are NOT auditing the order. You are auditing WHAT IS PRESENT.

Therefore:
  - NEVER report "the order is wrong", "this shot comes too early",
    "the sequence is confusing" or anything about narrative flow.
    Order is not a finding here. Not once.
  - NEVER assume shot number N is at position N.
  - Instead: identify every segment by WHAT IS IN IT, match it to the
    catalogue below, and judge each one against ITS OWN entry.


🚨 READ THIS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE — THIS PHASE IS INDOORS

Earlier phases of this film were outside in hard vacuum: one hard sun,
absolute black razor-edged shadows, a spacesuit helmet on every head.
NONE OF THAT APPLIES HERE. Every shot in this reel is INSIDE a
pressurised space station. If you carry the outdoor rules in, you will
file a page of findings that are all wrong. Specifically:

  ① THERE IS AIR IN HERE. Loose hair lifts and HOLDS a shape; light
    objects hang and drift. That is correct, not a physics error.
  ② THE SHADOWS ARE SOFT. The light comes from broad ceiling panels and
    small wall lamps — there is NO hard source anywhere in this phase.
    ⛔ A soft shadow is CORRECT here. A hard-edged black shadow is the
      finding, not the other way round.
  ③ SHE WEARS NO HELMET, IN ANY SHOT. Her head is bare throughout. That
    is correct and permanent; she never puts one on again.
  ④ SHE IS ALONE. There is no second person in this entire reel, in any
    shot, in any reflection, in any doorway. ONE entry has NO PEOPLE AT
    ALL. Neither is an error.
  ⑤ SHE NEVER SPEAKS. Not one line, not one moving lip, in any segment.
  ⑥ NO CAMERA MOVES. All twelve shots are locked off. There is not one
    push-in, pull-back, pan, tilt or track anywhere in this phase.

WHAT YOU HAVE

- One video: a number of separately generated shots laid end to end,
  16:9 horizontal, each roughly 8 seconds long, in scrambled order.
- The SHOT CATALOGUE below. It is ground truth, not a suggestion. Do not
  ask for a bible, a plan or a script.


⛔ STEP -1 — MATERIAL GATE AND GRID MEASUREMENT

Measure and write down:
    measured total duration           = ___ seconds
    number of distinct shot segments  = ___
    measured shot length = total / segment count = ___ seconds

The catalogue expects TWELVE shots. Ten to thirteen segments are all
plausible material — report the number you actually count.

If the measured duration is under 55 seconds, OR you count fewer than 7
segments, your ENTIRE output is this one line and nothing else:

    MATERIAL GATE FAILED - measured __ s, __ segments. Not the phase reel.

Then stop.

⭐ STATE THE MEASURED SHOT LENGTH EXPLICITLY. Also state whether the
individual segments are all the same length within 0.3 s, or differ.


⛔ STEP 0 — THE COMPILATION RULE, AND ZERO CAMERA MOVEMENT

This reel is a COMPILATION. A cut at every segment boundary is REQUIRED
BY THE ARCHITECTURE. NEVER report a cut, an angle change, a lens change,
a lighting change or a location change AT a segment boundary as an
error. Adjacent segments are supposed to look nothing alike.

DO report, at maximum severity, any cut, camera reset, angle jump,
sudden reframe or teleport that happens INSIDE a segment.

⭐ EVERY ONE OF THE TWELVE ENTRIES IS LOCKED OFF WITH ZERO CAMERA
  MOVEMENT. Unlike earlier phases there is no intended move anywhere.
  Any pan, tilt, track, zoom, drift, handheld float or shake inside any
  segment is a finding at high severity.

⛔ ONE TRAP: in entry D the WHOLE VIEW BEYOND A WINDOW slides steadily
  across the glass. That is NOT camera movement — it is the station
  turning, seen from inside, and it is the point of the shot. The window
  rim and the wall around it must stay perfectly still in frame. If the
  RIM moves, that IS a finding.


⛔ STEP 0.5 — HOW TO REFER TO THINGS

Number the segments by their POSITION IN THE FILE: Segment 1 is the
first one you see. Give each one its measured start and end timecode.
Each segment gets a CATALOGUE LETTER (A-L) once identified in STEP 2.
EVERY finding carries BOTH the segment number with its timecode AND the
catalogue letter, or it is deleted before you output it. Never use a
scene number like "S55".


⛔ STEP 1 — BUILD THE PASSPORT FROM THE VIDEO FIRST

Before comparing anything, write down what you ACTUALLY SEE:
    - her face and hair, in detail (see OPEN ITEM 1 — this is the most
      valuable thing in your report)
    - her suit, her wrists, her back
    - each separate space in the reel, and what distinguishes them
    - every window or pane, and what is on the other side of each
    - every light source, its position and its colour
    - the indicator lights: how many, how many lit, what colour

Only then compare against the catalogue. If video and spec disagree,
describe the VIDEO as it is and mark the deviation.


⛔ STEP 2 — IDENTIFY EVERY SEGMENT

For each segment decide which catalogue entry it is, using its CONTENT
SIGNATURE — which of the three spaces it is in, how close the shot is,
whether a face is visible, what is being touched, and what the lights
are doing.

For each segment produce one row:
    Segment N | start-end | catalogue letter | confidence high/low |
    the one detail that settled it

Then produce three lists, none of which may be skipped:
    MATCHED · MISSING · UNMATCHED (each unmatched one described in full)

⚠️ Four entries are close shots of a HAND ON A SURFACE with no face in
frame (C, F, J and part of D). They are easy to confuse with each other.
Separate them by WHAT the hand is touching and WHAT is around it.


================================================================
LOCKED SPEC - CONDITIONS THAT HOLD IN EVERY SHOT
================================================================

FORMAT
    Every shot: 16:9 horizontal, one unbroken take, ZERO internal cuts,
    ZERO camera movement.

AUDIO
    Every shot was generated SILENT. Breathing, room tone, the radio band
    and the station AI are added in post. Never grade lip-sync against
    the soundtrack, and never report "no audio" or "silent" as a defect.

⭐ THE PLACE - THREE SPACES, ONE STATION, ALL INDOORS
    ① THE AIRLOCK - a small cylindrical chamber with padded off-white
      walls. Entry A only.
    ② THE CORRIDOR - a straight cylindrical passage about two metres
      across, padded off-white, with handholds along it, a run of ceiling
      light panels, small amber wall lamps, a wall systems panel, a
      communications plate with a speaker grille and a recessed button,
      a SMALL ROUND PORTHOLE in the wall, and at one end a SEALED HATCH
      carrying a SMALL ROUND OBSERVATION WINDOW. Entries B, C, D, E, F,
      G, H, I.
    ③ THE RESCUE CAPSULE - a small cabin with padded walls, an indicator
      bank, a hatch with a long handle, and TWO EMPTY SEATS. Entries J,
      K, L.
    ⛔ There is NO exterior shot, NO vacuum shot of a person, NO planet
      surface and NO daylight anywhere in this phase.

⭐ THE TWO WINDOWS ARE NOT THE SAME WINDOW - AND THIS IS A REAL TEST
    THE PORTHOLE (entry D) is set in the corridor WALL and looks OUTSIDE:
      beyond it is the NIGHT side of a planet, nearly black, carrying
      scattered clusters and threads of small warm point lights.
    THE OBSERVATION WINDOW (entry E) is set in the SEALED HATCH and looks
      into a DEPRESSURISED MODULE: a dark room with a torn-open wall and
      loose objects hanging in it. No planet, no lights outside.
    ⛔ A window showing the planet where the dead module should be, or a
      dark room where the planet should be, is a finding at maximum
      severity. They are two different holes in two different walls.

⭐ LIGHT - SOFT, TWO COLOURS, AND IT STEPS UP EXACTLY ONCE
    KEY: cold white light from broad CEILING PANELS, flat and even,
      falling in a regular rhythm of pools along the corridor with dark
      gaps between them.
    FILL: small WARM AMBER emergency lamps low on the walls.
    ⛔ THERE IS NO HARD SOURCE ANYWHERE IN THIS PHASE. Every shadow is
      SOFT-EDGED. A hard-edged black shadow, a raking sunbeam, a visible
      light shaft or a lens flare is a finding. This is the opposite of
      the earlier phases and it is deliberate.
    ⭐ THE AMBER STEPS UP ONE LEVEL IN ENTRY I, ON SCREEN, and stays
      there. Its COLOUR does not change and nothing flashes or strobes.
      A pulsing, flashing or colour-shifting alarm light is a finding.
    ⭐ GREEN APPEARS IN EXACTLY ONE ENTRY: L, from an indicator bank
      below the frame, rising onto her face. Green anywhere else is a
      finding.
    NOTHING BURNS: no flame, no fire, no ember, no spark, no smoke, no
    steam, no vapour, no haze anywhere.

⭐ ZERO GRAVITY WITH AIR - the physical law of this phase
    There is air in here, and there is no gravity. Both are true at once:
      - Loose hair LIFTS away from the head and HOLDS a shape. It does
        not hang down, and it does not blow like wind. A person's hair
        lying flat on the head is a finding; hair streaming as if in a
        gale is a finding.
      - Loose objects HANG in the air and drift SLOWLY in DEAD STRAIGHT
        LINES at CONSTANT SPEED, turning at their own constant rates.
        Nothing accelerates, slows, settles, sinks or falls. An object
        that drifts downward and comes to rest is a finding at maximum
        severity.
      - She moves by PULLING on handholds. She never walks, never
        stands on a floor, never puts weight on her feet. A walking or
        standing figure is a finding at maximum severity.
      - When she pulls, her body travels in a straight line at a
        constant speed until she takes another hold. She does not coast
        to a stop on her own.

⭐ THE INVISIBLE LEAK - the one thing you must NOT see
    Air is escaping from this corridor into the sealed module. The film
    shows this with exactly ONE piece of evidence and NOTHING else:
      in entry G, a loose STRAP END beside the hatch seal is DRAWN OUT
      STRAIGHT toward one point on the seam and QUIVERS there, held, at
      one constant angle. It does not swing, does not fall, does not
      drift away.
    ⛔ THE LEAK IS NEVER DRAWN. No visible stream, no smoke, no steam, no
      vapour, no mist, no dust, no particles, no fog, no spray, no
      whistling jet of anything, in any entry. Any visible escaping gas
      is a finding at MAXIMUM severity — it destroys the only idea in
      the shot.

⭐ SCREEN DIRECTION - fixed for the whole phase
    In the corridor she always travels toward the SCREEN-LEFT. The
    capsule and the passage are to the screen-LEFT; the airlock she came
    from and the sealed hatch are to the screen-RIGHT. A shot in which
    she travels screen-right along the corridor is a finding.

⭐ THE COUNTER
    A six-segment amber ring is set into her LEFT wrist cuff. It reads
    TWO LIT AND FOUR DARK, and it does not change anywhere in this
    phase. It is legible in exactly one entry (F) and it is deliberately
    NOT emphasised there. A ring reading three or more lit is a finding.
    A steel watch on her RIGHT wrist is NOT visible in this phase.

TEXT - ZERO CHARACTERS, NO EXCEPTIONS
    This is an interior full of wall panels, indicator banks, buttons and
    hatches, and NONE of them carries a letter, digit, numeral, label,
    marking, gauge legend, icon, logo or insignia. Every indicator is an
    abstract coloured lamp with NO number beside it. Any readable
    character anywhere is a finding at maximum severity. This is the
    hardest lock in this phase to hold and you must check every panel.

SPEECH LOCK - SHE NEVER SPEAKS
    She has no lines anywhere in this phase. Her lips stay closed in
    every segment. Any visible lip movement, in any entry, is a finding
    at high severity. Two voices are heard in the finished film and both
    are machines, from ceiling and cabin speakers, with nobody on screen
    producing them.


================================================================
IDENTITY PASSPORT - THE WOMAN (flight engineer) - AND SHE IS ALONE
================================================================
    38 years old. 1.70 m, slim. Oval face, soft defined jawline, light
    wheat-toned skin with visible pores and visible fatigue, thin
    straight nose, dark hazel eyes. A short vertical pale scar on her

WHAT TO SCORE - thirteen categories, 1 to 10
================================================================

 1. COVERAGE - how much of the catalogue is present, and how confidently
    could each segment be identified? Score the material, not the order.
 2. ⭐ FACE CONTINUITY - is it the same face in every entry? Score this
    hard; it is the most consequential category in this audit.
 3. HEAD AND HAIR - bare head in all twelve, hair lifting and holding.
 4. COSTUME AND BACK - off-white suit with orange stripes, bare hands,
    her back flat with nothing on it, ring on the left wrist.
 5. ⭐ SOLITUDE AND FIGURE COUNT - one person in eleven entries, none in
    E, never two. Empty seats stay empty.
 6. CAMERA DISCIPLINE - all twelve locked off, zero movement.
 7. ⭐ LIGHT - soft sources only, cold white key and warm amber fill, the
    single amber step-up in I, green only in L, no hard shadow, no
    flare, no flashing.
 8. ZERO-G WITH AIR - hair lifting and holding, objects hanging and
    drifting straight at constant speed, nothing settling, no walking.
 9. ⭐ THE INVISIBLE LEAK - the strap in G doing its job, and NO visible
    gas anywhere in the reel.
10. THE TWO WINDOWS AND THE SPACES - correct thing beyond each window,
    the same corridor throughout, consistent screen direction.
11. THE PLANTED OBJECTS - the red cover closed and untouched in J, the
    panel lamps staying dark in F, the counter reading two.
12. TEXT DISCIPLINE - zero readable characters on any panel, button,
    cover or bank. Score this hard; an interior is where it fails.
13. DELIVERY HYGIENE - no title card, no end card, no credits, no
    captions, no watermark, no logo, no letterboxing; film grain present
    and consistent; segments the same length.

SEVERITY
    RED     blocker - the shot has to be regenerated
    YELLOW  fixable in the edit (trim, crop, speed, reframe)
    GREEN   note - worth knowing, not worth spending on

Scoring rules:
    - The overall score is the arithmetic mean of the thirteen category
      scores. Compute it from your own table.
    - A category with no observed problem is not automatically 10. Say
      what you verified.
    - Never give a category a number if you could not observe it. Write
      UNVERIFIABLE and leave it out of the mean.
    - ⛔ There is NO category for order, pacing, flow or structure.
```
