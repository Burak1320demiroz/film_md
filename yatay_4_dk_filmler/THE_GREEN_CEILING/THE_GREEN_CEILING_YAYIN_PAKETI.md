### DESCRIPTION (§2) — film-specific lines only

```
A storm on a heavy-air world does not blow sideways. It lifts.

Two crew work a cargo pickup under a closed forest canopy. The light overhead
starts going out, and the ground stops holding what they put down.

THE SCIENCE
• Earth's own updrafts lift more than people think: the US record hailstone (Vivian, South Dakota, 23 July 2010) was 20 cm across and weighed 0.88 kg, and holding a stone that size aloft takes an updraft well over 100 mph. (NOAA/NWS)
• Drag scales with air density — F = ½ρv²C_dA — so the wind needed to lift something scales as the square root of gravity over density. In air three times as thick at 0.42 g, 10 m/s does the work 27.5 m/s does here.
• Titan is the measured case: Huygens found about 1.45 bar of atmosphere in 0.14 g, roughly four times denser than ours, dense enough that NASA has noted a person with wings could fly in it. (ESA/NASA Cassini-Huygens, 2005)
```

```
PINNED (§5):
   Storms on Earth already lift things — that is what hail is. A hailstone
   only grows because the updraft keeps throwing it back up through the wet
   part of the cloud, and the 0.88 kg stone that fell on Vivian, South Dakota
   in July 2010 needed a column of rising air well over 100 mph to stay there.
   What we changed is not the storm, it is the air. Lift goes with density and
   against gravity — v = √(2mg / ρC_dA) — so in air about three times as thick
   as ours at 0.42 g, a wind of 10 m/s carries what would take 27.5 m/s at
   home. Nothing here is anti-gravity; it is Archimedes and drag in a heavier
   fluid, which is why the dust still settles while the flat plates go up. The
   choice in the film follows from that and nothing else: at some point the
   only mass you control is the mass you are carrying.
   Real question — if the air will only let two of the three leave, which one
   do you open your hand on: the one you can replace, or the one you spent the
   most getting?
```
