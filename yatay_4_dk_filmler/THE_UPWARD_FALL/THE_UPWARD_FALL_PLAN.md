A forest planet. The air is 3.2 atmospheres and unbreathable. Gravity is

and climbs in a straight vertical line toward the nearest pale gap in the
canopy directly above it, at about 0.6 metres per second — continuous and
steady, never stationary, never hovering, never drifting sideways, never
spinning. Large dead masses climb more slowly, about 0.35 metres per
second. Living tissue stays down and behaves normally: a living leaf that
is torn off falls to the ground.

THE CANOPY ANIMAL: one large animal lives in the canopy and takes what
rises. It never comes to the ground and it never walks. It has one body
about four metres across and four long limbs, each about nine metres,
and it moves on one axis only — down and up. It has no legs, no head, no
eyes and no visible mouth. It is grey-green like the bark.

THE GAP: a rift forty metres wide cuts the forest floor. A dead trunk
sixty metres long and nine metres thick lies fallen across it and is the
only way over. Five thick living roots, each as wide as a man's chest, run
from the far bank over the trunk and down into the floor, holding it. On
the far side, four hundred metres away, a small steady light stands where
the lander is.
