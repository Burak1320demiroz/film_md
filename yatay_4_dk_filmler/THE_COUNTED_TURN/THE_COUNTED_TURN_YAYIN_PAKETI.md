### DESCRIPTION (§2) — film-specific lines only

```
Walk one way around a spinning ring and you get heavier. Walk the other way
and you get lighter. She has one cable run left.

A rigger works alone on the inner deck of a ring station that is being spun up
to its final rate. Every turn the ring takes, everything on it weighs a little
more — including the cable she is laying, and the clips holding it down.

THE SCIENCE
• Apparent weight inside a rotating habitat comes from total tangential speed.
  Walking with the spin adds to it; walking against it subtracts. At the ring's
  own speed, against the spin, a body weighs nothing at all.
• Anything you drop inside a spinning ring lands to one side. The offset is set
  by the radius and the drop height and nothing else — spin it faster and the
  object still lands in the same place, it just gets there sooner.
• Rotating habitat design guidance sits near 1-2 rpm. This ring runs 3 to 5.
  Nobody here is adapted to it.
• There is no sound in vacuum, but a ring is a bell. In aluminium, sound moves
  at about 5,100 m/s, so every touch travels the whole circle and comes back.

CHAPTERS
0:00 What falls sideways
0:09 The count starts
0:29 One clip is enough
0:59 The far side
1:59 Above her, the same deck
2:09 Halfway
2:09 The answer to the rule
```
