# 🎼 §A — MÜZİK

## A.1 · ⭐ ANA PARÇA PROMPTU (tek parça, 249 saniye)

> **Kural (§14-1):** Müzik TEK PARÇA üretilir, sahne sahne değil. Her
> sahneye ayrı parça üretmek filmi müzik kliplerinin toplamına çevirir.
> 25 klibi tek film yapan yegâne bağ budur.

```
A single continuous 4 minute 10 second instrumental cinematic score for a
science-fiction survival film set on a desert planet. 72 BPM, strict and
unwavering, 4/4, D minor with a lowered second degree — a Phrygian colour
that never quite settles. [Instrumental] [No Vocals]

CORE IDENTITY: This is not threat music and not grief music. It is CLOCK
music — patient, mineral, indifferent. The planet is not hostile; it is
simply continuing. The pulse must stay exactly the same when the worst
thing in the film happens.

LAYERS, in order of entry:
A low sustained drone at 44 Hz, doubled by quiet companion tones one and
one-and-a-half octaves higher at roughly 88 and 132 Hz, so the weight
survives on a phone speaker; the 44 Hz fundamental adds depth on
headphones but is never the only carrier of the emotion.
A dry granular texture of fine mineral grains in the 2-6 kHz band,
constantly moving but never rhythmic — it must NOT lock to the pulse and
must never sound like rain, water or fire.
A single dry struck-stone tone, low and woody, sounding ONCE PER BAR —
one hit every 3.3 seconds. No drum kit, no snare, no hi-hat, no
percussion groove of any kind. This single tone is the clock.
A solo acoustic cello, close-miked and unsentimental — the only human
timbre in the piece, used sparingly.
A high bowed-metal tone, thin and sustained, entering only in the second
half — the storm's voice, never a swell, never a hit.

STRUCTURE:
Bars 1-0: an unmetered opening drone of about five seconds with no pulse
at all, then the struck-stone clock begins and never stops until the
final silence.
The first third: drone, grains and clock only. Cold, wide, spacious.
Around one minute twenty seconds the cello enters with a rising
three-note phrase and STOPS ON THE SECOND NOTE. The phrase does not
resolve. It is left hanging for roughly forty seconds, then the missing
third note finally arrives one octave BELOW where the ear expects it.
The middle third: the high bowed-metal tone enters underneath and grows
by tiny amounts, never in a swell, never in a crescendo of drums.
At about two minutes fifty seconds every layer stops mid-phrase and the
track goes to complete silence for twelve seconds. Nothing fades; it just
stops. The silence is part of the composition.
It returns with one single low cello note, alone, and rebuilds slowly.
At about three minutes thirty seconds the piece resolves — ONCE, the only
resolution in the whole track — and the resolution falls DOWNWARD into
the low octave instead of rising. Relief, not triumph.
The final thirty seconds thin back out to drone, grains and clock.
The track is CUT mid-phrase at four minutes seven seconds. No fade out,
no final chord, no cadence, no ritardando, no cymbal swell.

MIX: dry and close. Very short early reflections, no reverb tail — an
open desert has nothing to reflect off. Stereo image wide but
mono-compatible. Mastered around -17 LUFS.

DO NOT: no vocals, no choir, no lyrics, no drum kit, no trap hi-hats, no
808s, no epic trailer braams, no rising risers, no orchestral swell, no
timpani rolls, no bell tree, no whoosh transitions, no fade out, no
triumphant ending, no key change, no tempo change, no synced sound
effects, no water or rain textures, no wind sound effects inside the
music.
```


## B.2 · Yatak üretim promptu (Suno/ElevenLabs SFX/Freesound katmanı)

```
A single continuous 4 minute 15 second ambient sound bed for a desert
planet, no music, no melody, no rhythm, no instruments. [SFX only]

Dry granular wind moving over fine sand: broadband hiss between 200 Hz
and 6 kHz with audible individual grain collisions inside it, never
smooth, never watery. Beneath it a very low slow-breathing rumble
between 60 and 140 Hz, distant and massive, doubled by a quiet companion
tone around 130 Hz so it survives on small speakers. Above it sparse
irregular ticks of sand grains striking a hard helmet shell, 3 to 12 per
second, close and dry and unrhythmic. Under everything a constant faint
narrow-band hum around 300 Hz, like a small fan inside a sealed helmet.

The bed swells very slowly across the whole duration as a storm front
approaches, from thin and distant to close and heavy, and never once
drops to silence.

DO NOT: no music, no melody, no drums, no pulse, no rain, no ocean, no
surf, no waves, no bubbles, no fire, no crackling flames, no thunder
claps, no voices, no birds, no insects, no engine, no radio chatter, no
whoosh transitions, no risers, no fade to silence at any point.
```
