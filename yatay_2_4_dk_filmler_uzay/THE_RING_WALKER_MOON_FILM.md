## 🌍 WORLD BIBLE — UYDU: KASSİA (Vael-7b)

```
CLASSIFICATION: A mid-sized rocky moon (diameter: ~3,400km, comparable
to Earth's Moon) orbiting Vael-7, a warm amber-ochre gas giant 14 times
the mass of Jupiter. Kassia is tidally locked — the same face always
points toward Vael-7. The explored hemisphere (Vael-facing side) sees
the gas giant filling 35 degrees of sky and the ring system arcing
overhead in a permanent, unchanging band.

CRITICAL WORLD RULE — THE RINGS: Vael-7's ring system spans 280,000km
in diameter. From Kassia's surface, the rings appear as a massive,
brilliant arc stretching from horizon to horizon across the full width
of the sky, approximately 60 degrees wide at their thickest point
overhead. The rings are composed of ice, metallic rock fragments, and
silicate dust — they catch the system's star's light and reflect it
downward onto Kassia's surface as a diffused, warm silver-gold ambient
glow. The rings are NOT uniformly bright — they have dense bands (bright,
opaque) and sparse Cassini-division-like gaps (darker, more transparent,
through which the star field is visible). The rings appear to rotate
VERY SLOWLY — imperceptibly in a 10-second shot, but visibly different
over an hour. For all practical filming purposes, the rings are STATIC.

THE METALLIC GLASS SAND: Kassia's entire surface is covered in a deep
layer (5-50cm) of fine metallic glass sand — officially called regolith
fulgur, but visually indistinguishable from very fine, pale silver-gold
glass beads. This sand was formed over billions of years as ring
particles (primarily metalite, a naturally occurring iron-silicate glass)
fell onto Kassia's surface, accumulated, and were slowly ground into
fine particulate by micrometeorite impacts. Each grain is 0.1-1mm in
diameter, highly spherical, and acts as a tiny prism — sunlight and
ring-light enter each grain and exit as a spectrum of colors. The
overall surface appears pale silver-gold at rest, but when disturbed
(by footsteps, wind, or impacts), individual grains catch light and
produce brief, multi-colored prism flashes that last 1-2 seconds
before the disturbed grains settle. A footstep leaves a sharp, clear
impression (the grains are dense enough to hold shape) and the disturbed
zone sparkles with brief prism flashes for ~3 seconds before settling.

THE METEORITE RAIN: Ring particles occasionally drift into Kassia's
gravitational influence and enter the thin atmosphere, producing a
constant, low-level meteorite shower visible from the surface. These
are NOT dangerous bolides — they are small (1-20cm original size),
and most burn up completely in the thin atmosphere, producing brief,
bright streaks of white-amber light. Approximately 3-8 streaks are
visible per minute — enough that the sky always appears "active" with
silent, graceful falling lines. Occasionally (every 5-10 minutes) a
slightly larger particle (20-50cm) survives to impact the surface —
producing a sharp crack sound and a small metallic glass splash crater
within 200m-2km of the observer. These are visible but non-threatening.

THE STAR: Kassia's host star (called Aru) is a warm K-type orange
dwarf — slightly cooler and oranger than Earth's sun, producing a
warm, slightly amber-tinted daylight. Combined with the ring's silver-
white reflected light, the surface illumination has a warm amber-silver
dual-source quality that is distinctive and cinematic. The star is
always visible in the sky unless behind Vael-7 (ring shadow — a 6-hour
eclipse every orbit).

THE GAS GIANT (Vael-7): On the Vael-facing side, Vael-7 fills 35
degrees of sky — a warm amber-ochre sphere with visible cloud band
stripes (caramel, dark amber, cream) and swirling storm systems. It
does NOT fill the entire sky — it occupies the upper-center of the
visible sky, flanked by the ring system arcing away in both directions.
Vael-7 is not the primary light source (the star Aru is brighter)
but it does contribute warm reflected amber light to the surface,
creating a secondary warm fill from the upper sky direction.

GRAVITY: 0.38g — significantly lower than Earth. Footsteps are longer
and slightly bouncier (like the Moon but with more control). Jumping
is effortless. Dust and disturbed sand particles take longer to settle.
Meteorite impacts produce ejecta that travels farther and settles more
slowly than on Earth.

ATMOSPHERE: Very thin — 0.01 atm, not breathable. Composed primarily
of nitrogen and trace argon. Too thin to produce audible wind on most
days, but carries sound faintly at close range (within 5m). Occasional
"ring shadow" days (when Kassia passes through Vael-7's shadow) drop
surface temperature sharply — visible as surface frost forming on the
metallic glass sand.

SOUND: Near-silence. The thin atmosphere carries the faintest whispers
of sound at close range — boot crunching in glass sand (a distinct,
crystalline, high-frequency sound), suit ventilation systems, and the
sharp crack of a meteorite impact (audible but thin and dry, not
resonant). No wind sound unless a meteorite impact creates brief air
disturbance.
```

## 👤 CHARACTER BIBLE — DR. EDA ÇELİK

```
IDENTITY: 33-year-old Turkish planetary geologist specializing in ring
system dynamics and impact mineralogy — the study of minerals formed
by meteorite impacts. She designed this mission herself after detecting
anomalous spectroscopic readings in Kassia's surface material: the
metallic glass sand should not contain the specific iron-silicate
compound she found in remote sensing data. The compound matches the
predicted composition of Vael-7's innermost ring — a ring that models
predict will have fully decayed into the planet's atmosphere within
500,000 years. She is standing on a surface made of a ring that is
slowly dying. The sand beneath her feet is a clock counting down.

FACE (photographed, not rendered): Warm olive-tan skin with visible
pore texture and natural subsurface scattering. A round face with
wide, expressive dark brown eyes that reflect light sources accurately
— ring light reflects as a warm silver-gold crescent. Strong, arched
eyebrows. A small, slightly upturned nose. Full lips, often slightly
parted when she is thinking — a visible tell of her analytical mind
at work. A constellation of faint freckles across the bridge of her
nose (from fieldwork sun exposure). A small vertical scar through her
left eyebrow — from a field accident two years ago. No airbrushing,
no plastic smoothness, no artificial symmetry.

HAIR: Dark brown, thick, shoulder-length. Worn loose inside the helmet,
slightly wavy, tucked behind her ears. Several strands fall across her
forehead and are pressed against the visor interior in zero-atmosphere
static electricity — a subtle detail visible in close shots.

BUILD: 1.67m, sturdy and practical — broad shoulders, strong hands,
the build of someone who spends months in the field. She moves
efficiently in low gravity — she has worked in 0.38g before and knows
not to overcommit to steps or jumps.

SUIT — DEEP GEOLOGY SURVEY SUIT (DGS-6):
- base color: deep teal-indigo (a rich, dark blue-green, not black —
  reads as dark indigo in shade, deep teal in direct amber light)
- ACCENT PANELS: copper-gold reinforced panels along the outer arms
  (forearm to upper arm), outer shins and boots, and the helmet ridge —
  designed for visibility in remote field operations. The copper-gold
  color provides high contrast against the silver-gold sand and the
  teal suit body.
- HELMET: low-profile, rounded. Deep teal-indigo with a copper-gold
  band running horizontally above the visor. A fully TRANSPARENT,
  seamless visor that always reveals her complete face. No HUD elements
  visible on the visor. The visor has a faint anti-static coating
  visible as a very subtle iridescence when light hits at oblique angles.
- HEADLAMP: A single wide-beam headlamp above the visor center (useful
  inside shadows and craters — not needed in direct daylight).
- CHEST: Four small sample indicator LEDs arranged vertically on the
  left chest — they glow faintly amber when her sample collection
  compartments are in use (begins after Scene 5).
- WRIST DISPLAY: Left forearm, showing TWO abstract readouts only:
  a slow pulsing amber ring (suit integrity — steady = stable,
  irregular = warning) and a shifting color spectrum bar (spectrometer
  reading — what minerals is the surface emitting?). NEVER shows
  text, numbers, or letters.
- GLOVES: Reinforced, slim-profile, deep teal with copper-gold grip
  pads on the fingertips and palm. Designed for precise sample handling.
- BOOTS: Broad-tread, copper-gold soles, deep teal uppers. The broad
  treads are designed to compress into the glass sand without sinking
  too deeply (0.38g means she sinks less than on Earth anyway).
- SAMPLE COLLECTOR: A low-profile pouch on her right thigh and a
  small rigid sample case clipped to her left hip — the case is
  silver-grey, approximately 20cm × 10cm, visible in full-body shots.
- TETHER: A short, retractable safety tether on her right hip —
  clips to anchor points in her lander for steep terrain.

SUIT CONTINUITY RULE [LESSON 8]: Every detail above remains IDENTICAL
across all scenes. Dust accumulates on boot soles and lower shins from
Scene 2 onward (metallic glass sand is distinctive: silver-gold). The
sample case goes from empty/flat (Scenes 0-4) to visibly full (Scenes
5-10). These are the ONLY changes — no suit repairs, no color shifts.

VISOR PERSISTENCE RULE [LESSON 5]: Visor ALWAYS VISIBLE in every frame
regardless of head angle. The iridescent anti-static coating visible
at oblique angles.

FACE LOCK [LESSON 16]: Face maintains IDENTICAL bone structure, eye
spacing, nose shape, freckle constellation, left eyebrow scar throughout.

CONTACT INTEGRITY RULE [LESSON 11]: When touching ANY surface (sand,
rock, lander hull, sample cases), her hands/fingers maintain COMPLETE
STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY — clear visible boundary at all contact points.

MOVEMENT SIGNATURE: Efficient, ground-covering, confident in low-g.
She takes slightly longer strides than Earth-normal (0.38g encourages
it) but keeps her center of gravity low. She crouches to examine sand
with focused precision — fingertips working, not hands. When she looks
up at the ring system, she always tilts her head slowly — she has seen
it hundreds of times but it never stops compelling her attention. Her
signature gesture: touching the left eyebrow scar briefly when she
encounters something unexpected. A habit she is unaware of.
```

## 💡 LIGHTING BIBLE

```
THIS FILM'S LIGHTING ARCHITECTURE: Three light sources, always active
simultaneously. Understanding their interaction is essential to every
prompt:

LIGHT SOURCE 1 — ARU (The Star, warm orange-white, 5200K):
The primary light source. A warm, slightly orange-tinted star similar
to a late-afternoon Earth sun in intensity. Casts DEFINED shadows
(sharp-edged, not diffused). Light direction varies by scene position
(generally coming from upper-right or upper-left). Creates warm
highlights on the copper-gold suit accents — they glow like fire.
Creates warm amber highlights on the ring system's dense bands.

LIGHT SOURCE 2 — RING REFLECTED LIGHT (cool silver-white, 5800K):
The ring system reflects Aru's light downward onto Kassia's surface
as a diffused, omnidirectional fill light — cooler in color temperature
than Aru, coming from the full arc of the sky rather than a single
direction. This is a SOFT FILL light — it reduces shadows slightly
and adds a silver-white tint to surfaces in shade. The ring light
is what gives this film its distinctive dual-temperature look:
warm amber highlights (Aru) + cool silver-white shadows and fill
(rings). This is NOT identical to any of the previous 25 films.

LIGHT SOURCE 3 — VAEL-7 REFLECTED LIGHT (warm amber, 4000K):
The gas giant reflects warm amber light from the upper sky. This is
the WEAKEST of the three sources — a warm amber fill from above,
most noticeable in scenes where Vael-7 is prominent in frame. It
adds subtle amber warmth to the tops of helmets and shoulders.

LIGHTING INTERACTION RULE: The three light sources create a distinctive
"triple temperature" quality on every surface — warm amber (Aru on lit
surfaces), cool silver (ring fill on shade), warm amber (Vael-7 on top).
This means SHADOWS ARE NEVER PURE BLACK — they are silver-grey, filled
by ring light. This film has NO deep shadows in outdoor scenes.

PHASE 1 — SURFACE (Scenes 0-3, 10): All three sources active. Aru is
the key light, rings are the fill, Vael-7 is a rim light from above.
The metallic glass sand sparkles under all three sources simultaneously.

PHASE 2 — CRATER RIM AND INTERIOR (Scenes 4-6): Inside a large impact
crater, Aru may be behind the crater wall — ring light becomes the
dominant source (cool silver-white dominant), with Vael-7 providing
the only warm accent from directly above. The crater interior has
a distinctly cooler, more dramatic lighting quality.

PHASE 3 — RING SHADOW ECLIPSE (Scene 7-8): Kassia passes through
Vael-7's shadow — Aru disappears behind the gas giant. Only ring
reflected light (now illuminated by Aru from the opposite side —
even cooler, more blue-white) and Vael-7's own glow (now the only
warm source) illuminate the surface. The eclipse is NOT total
darkness — the rings glow beautifully in this configuration, and
the surface frost forms visibly as temperature drops.

ANTI-BLOOM DIRECTIVE [LESSON 3]: The ring system, Vael-7, and all
reflected light sources must be SHARP and HIGH-CONTRAST. The ring
system is NOT a soft glowing band — it has defined edges, visible
particle density variations, distinct gap structures. Metallic glass
sand prism flashes are SHARP, not diffused. No bloom, no glow filter,
no soft focus haze on any light source.

MOTIVATED LIGHTING RULE: Every light in every frame has a visible,
traceable source. No ambient fill from unknown origins.

COLOR TEMPERATURE NARRATIVE:
- Scenes 0-3: Triple source (amber + silver + amber top) — rich, complex
- Scenes 4-6: Cool dominant (ring fill in crater) — more dramatic
- Scenes 7-8: Eclipse (cool silver-blue + warm amber accent) — most dramatic
- Scenes 9-10: Return to triple source — synthesis and resolution
```

## ⚙️ PHYSICS BIBLE

```
GRAVITY: 0.38g (Moon-like). Visible effects in every moving shot:
- Boot impacts in sand produce a slight delay in full depression
  (less weight compressing the sand)
- Dust and disturbed sand particles take 2-3x longer to fully settle
  than on Earth — they drift and float slightly
- Dr. Çelik's strides are 10-15% longer than Earth-normal
- Any dropped object falls with a visible low-g arc — reaching the
  ground 2-3x more slowly than on Earth
- Meteorite ejecta from impact craters travels in long, flat arcs

METEORITE RAIN PHYSICS: Meteorites enter the thin atmosphere at high
velocity and decelerate rapidly. They appear as bright streaks (warm
white to amber) that last 0.5-2 seconds. The streak angle is generally
slightly off-vertical (ring material orbits at an angle). 3-8 visible
per minute = at least one visible in every 10-second scene. They are
SILENT from a distance — the atmosphere is too thin to carry sound
except for nearby impacts. They NEVER explode or fragment visibly —
they simply burn to nothing, or occasionally impact the surface with
a sharp crack.

METALLIC GLASS SAND PHYSICS:
- Each grain 0.1-1mm, spherical, dense metalite glass
- Footstep impact compresses sand 3-5cm; boot impression is CRISP and
  holds shape (grains interlock)
- Disturbed grains catch light and produce brief prism flashes (individual
  colored points — not a diffuse glow, but specific individual flashes
  of different colors: red, amber, green, blue, violet) lasting 1-3 seconds
- When sand is scooped or kicked, individual grains arc slowly in 0.38g
  and land with tiny individual flashes
- Wind (rare on Kassia) would produce a low-level continuous shimmer
  across the entire surface

DYNAMIC SAND RULE [adapting LESSON 10]: Any disturbed metallic glass
sand must show physically accurate settling behavior under 0.38g.
Grains scatter in long, flat arcs and settle slowly. Prism flashes at
disturbed zones are SHARP and INDIVIDUAL — not a diffuse glow, but
distinct points of different colors. NEVER frozen, static, or uniform.

COLLISION INTEGRITY [LESSON 17]: All objects obey rigid-body collision.
Boots press into sand (not through it). Sample cases clip to suit (not
phase through). Meteorite fragments rest ON surfaces.

VOLUMETRIC PARTICLES [LESSON 10]: Any dust or fine sand dispersal from
footsteps or meteorite impacts must disperse volumetrically in 0.38g —
slower settling, wider arc, no frozen static clouds.

GRAVITY VECTOR [LESSON 15]: Hair presses against visor interior (static
electricity in thin atmo). Loose sand grains fall at 0.38g rate — visibly
slower than Earth. The lander's solar panel shadows are correctly angled.

ENVIRONMENT STABILITY [LESSON 6]: The ring system, gas giant, and distant
landscape remain COMPLETELY STATIC unless explicitly described as changing.
The rings do not breathe, warp, or morph. Meteorite streaks are the ONLY
moving background element — and they are brief, directional, and physically
consistent.

Human scale references: Dr. Çelik 1.67m; Şavci lander 12m long;
ring system begins 50,000km altitude (fills sky but is distant);
Vael-7 disc at 35° sky coverage; meteorite streaks typically 2-15km
altitude; surface sand depth 5-50cm over hard basalt.
```

## 🚫 NEGATIVE BIBLE

```
No cuts, no transitions, no dissolves, no fades, no jump cuts, no
montage, no editing, no multiple camera angles, no scene changes, no
camera teleportation. No shaky camera, no handheld look, no video game
chase-cam. Not miniature, not toy-like. No CGI look, no Unreal Engine
aesthetic, no video game aesthetic. No airbrushed or plastic skin. No
unnatural perfect facial symmetry. No oversaturated colors.

No bloom, no glow filter, no soft focus on ANY light source — the ring
system has SHARP, defined edges, not a soft glowing band. Prism flashes
in metallic glass sand are SHARP INDIVIDUAL POINTS of color, not a
diffuse glow. Aru (the star) is a clear, defined light source with hard
shadows, not a soft hazy glow.

No ambient fill from unknown sources — all light comes from Aru,
the ring system reflection, or Vael-7 reflection. Shadows are filled
by ring light (silver-grey), never pure black in outdoor scenes.

No meteorite explosions — meteorites burn and streak, they do not
explode in large fireballs (they are small). No dramatic impact shockwaves
for distant meteorite hits.

No text, digits, letters, or symbols on ANY display or surface — abstract
pulsing light and color spectrum bar only. No face morphing. No hand-
surface merging. No terrain warping. No frozen particles. No static sand
after disturbance. No uniform sand color — metallic glass sand sparkles
with individual prism flashes when disturbed.

No horror tone, no survival tension. This is discovery and contemplation.
Dr. Çelik is not in danger — she is at work. The meteorite rain is
background weather, not a threat.

VISOR PERSISTENCE [LESSON 5]: Visor ALWAYS VISIBLE.
FACE LOCK [LESSON 16]: Identical facial structure throughout.
SUIT CONTINUITY [LESSON 8]: Sample case accumulates contents only.
NO AI TEXT [LESSON 13]: All displays show abstract readouts only.
CONTACT INTEGRITY [LESSON 11]: Fingers/hands clear of all surfaces.
REFLECTION ACCURACY [LESSON 19]: Visor reflects actual scene accurately.
GRAVITY VECTOR [LESSON 15]: 0.38g sand settling, no Earth-speed falls.
ENVIRONMENT STABILITY [LESSON 6]: Rings, gas giant, horizon are static.
VOLUMETRIC PARTICLES [LESSON 10]: Sand disturbance volumetric, 0.38g.
DYNAMIC SAND [adapted LESSON 14]: No frozen or static disturbed sand.
PARTICLE GRAVITY [LESSON 30]: ALL loose particles obey 0.38g. No floating,
no liquefying, no anti-gravity. Particles pour downward when tilted.
IMPACT PHYSICS [LESSON 31]: Meteorite impacts show terrain deformation,
mass-consistent ejecta, physical density. No CGI particle spray.
```
