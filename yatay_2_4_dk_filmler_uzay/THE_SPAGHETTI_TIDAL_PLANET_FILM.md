## 🌍 WORLD BIBLE — GEZEGEN: LACRIMA

```
ORBITAL SITUATION: A rocky planet in an extremely tight, tidally-locked orbit around
a stellar-mass black hole named KARANOKS. The planet has survived at this distance
for millions of years in a stable but extreme tidal equilibrium — close enough for
severe differential gravity, not close enough for true disintegration.

PLANETARY SHAPE: Not a sphere. LACRIMA has been permanently deformed by eons of tidal
stress into a subtle teardrop / egg shape, elongated along the axis pointing toward
Karanoks. The elongation is gradual and geological — visible in landscape features,
not in a single dramatic bulge.

TIDAL PHYSICS (governs every scene):
- Gravity is NOT uniform. The side of the planet facing Karanoks (the "substellar
  point") experiences the strongest stretching force, pulling matter gently outward
  toward the black hole. The far side experiences a secondary, weaker outward pull
  in the opposite direction. The terminator zones between them experience compression.
- On a human body standing anywhere near the substellar region, feet (closer to the
  pull) experience measurably more gravitational force than the head — producing a
  faint, constant sensation of being gently stretched, like a light, continuous pull
  from the ankles.
- This same differential force elongates shadows, dropped objects' fall-arcs, and
  hanging cables on the substellar-facing side — they stretch fractionally longer
  and thinner than physics would allow on a normal world, always oriented toward
  Karanoks.
- Over geological time this same force has permanently stretched the planet's own
  mountain ranges into thin, elongated ridges — like pulled taffy frozen in rock —
  all oriented along the tidal axis, pointing toward the black hole.
- The planet's single ocean is permanently, visibly tilted — its surface holds a
  faint, constant slope toward the substellar horizon rather than lying flat, held
  in place by the balance of tidal pull and the planet's own gravity.

ATMOSPHERE: Thin but breathable-pressure equivalent for suit purposes; suits are
worn for tidal-stress protection and radiation shielding, not for lack of air. Wind
exists but is gentle — a slow, low atmospheric drift always flowing toward the
substellar horizon, never violent.

SURFACE CONDITION: Ancient, tidally-weathered rock — smoothed and elongated rather
than cratered. Fine mineral dust drifts slowly in the same direction as the wind,
settling in wind-ward streaks. Thin, stretched fracture lines run parallel to the
tidal axis across every rock surface.

SKY AS CHARACTER: The black hole Karanoks is a permanent, dominant feature of the
sky in tidal-axis-facing scenes — not visible as a black disc alone, but surrounded
by:
- a thin, brilliant accretion disk of superheated matter, glowing white-gold at its
  inner edge, fading to deep amber-red at its outer edge
- a visible photon ring — a razor-thin, warped halo of light bent almost fully around
  the event horizon itself
- gravitational lensing that bends and duplicates background starlight into smeared,
  arcing streaks and faint mirrored star-clusters around the black hole's silhouette
- no accretion-disk sound (vacuum-adjacent thin atmosphere) — all such events are
  visually silent

LIGHT BEHAVIOR: The accretion disk functions as the planet's primary "sun" — its
light is harsh, white-gold, and directional, always coming from the tidal-axis
direction. Combined with a distant true sun (small, pale, secondary light source),
this creates long, doubled shadows on the substellar-facing terrain, one warm-white
from the disk, one paler and cooler from the true sun.

SOUND: Thin atmosphere permits faint sound — wind is audible as a low, constant,
directional hush, but there is no thunder, no violent atmospheric noise.
```

## 👤 CHARACTER BIBLE — DR. DERİN AKMAN

```
IDENTITY: 33-year-old Turkish gravitational and tidal-force physicist. First and
only human explorer certified for surface operations this close to an active black
hole. Weeks into a tidal-equilibrium research assignment.

FACE (photographed, not rendered): Warm olive-tan skin with realistic visible pore
texture and natural subsurface scattering under harsh accretion-disk light — no
airbrushing, no plastic smoothness. A strong, slightly asymmetric jawline. Deep-set
dark brown eyes with visible catchlight, faint natural crow's-feet at the corners
from squinting against bright light for years. A slightly crooked nose (an old,
faded break, barely visible). Full lips, naturally chapped at the edges. A thin,
faded scar along her left temple, mostly hidden by hair. Subtle natural facial
asymmetry — a real human face, not a symmetric CGI construction.

HAIR: Dark brown, thick, cut into a short, practical undercut with slightly longer
top-length swept back, a few realistic loose strands escaping near her temple where
it meets the helmet seal.

BUILD: Athletic, slightly compact, 1.66m, a deliberate low center of gravity in her
stance and gait — trained posture for operating in a variable-gravity field. Slight
forward lean when walking, as though perpetually bracing.

SUIT: Deep slate-blue "tidal-compensating" exoskeletal suit, visibly mission-worn:
- reinforced articulated joints at ankles, knees, and hips (visible internal tension
  cabling beneath a semi-transparent joint casing) — engineered specifically to
  counteract differential pull on limbs
- scuffed boot soles, fine mineral dust ground into every seam
- faint burn-scoring near the left shoulder from an old equipment malfunction
- a stenciled callsign, half-faded, on her right forearm plate
- only faint, aged pale-gold accent lines (echoing the accretion disk's color) rather
  than bright glowing lines
- a fully transparent, seamless helmet always revealing her complete face, with
  faint condensation near the seal from breath in the cooler open air
- HANDS ALWAYS EMPTY — no handheld tools, wands, or scanners. All readings come from
  a single scratched, well-used wrist display on her left forearm. Any calibration
  object used (Scene 4.5) deploys from a small wrist-mounted dispenser, not held as
  a separate tool.

MOVEMENT SIGNATURE: A trained tidal-physicist's economical stance — slightly wider
footing than normal, weight held low, small pauses before shifting weight during
transitions (adjusting instinctively to the local differential pull). A faint,
constant awareness in her posture that her own body is being gently pulled unevenly.
```

## 🚀 SHIP BIBLE — TIDAL-STABILIZED RESEARCH VESSEL _MERİDYEN_

```
HULL LENGTH: 420 meters, stem to stern. Always anchored to this figure or a visible
reference object — never described only as "huge" or "massive."

PURPOSE-DRIVEN DESIGN: Unlike a standard cruiser, Meridyen's hull is asymmetrically
reinforced along its tidal axis — the black-hole-facing end is visibly thicker and
more heavily plated than the trailing end, a design response to constant differential
gravitational stress during approach and landing.

STRUCTURE (in countable parts, never one smooth mass):
- a reinforced, wedge-shaped forward section (facing the tidal axis), 140 meters long,
  plated in dark, heat-scored armor
- a slimmer, lighter aft section, 280 meters long, housing crew and engine decks
- hundreds of small reinforced observation windows, each approximately 1.2 meters
  in diameter, running the aft section's length as a built-in scale ruler
- four counter-thrust engine nacelles (not two, for tidal-compensation redundancy),
  each 10 meters in diameter, continuously micro-adjusting thrust to hold stable
  orbit and landing position against the tidal gradient
- sixteen-meter-tall reinforced landing struts with visible hydraulic tension dampers
- one forward cockpit window, 2 meters high by 3.5 meters wide, deeply recessed into
  the armored wedge nose

SCALE ANCHORS REQUIRED IN EVERY SHIP SHOT: at least one of — the 16m struts against
a visible boulder field, the 2m cockpit window with a human figure inside at correct
proportion, Derin's 1.66m figure against the hull, or a visible elongated mountain
ridge in the background for landscape-scale comparison.

MANDATORY QUALIFIER IN EVERY SHIP PROMPT: "physically accurate perspective, correct
engineering proportions, realistic scale relationships — the ship must never appear
miniature, toy-like, or like a compact spacecraft."

SHOT-SPLIT RULE: A single frame never simultaneously demands (a) the full 420m hull
in view AND (b) a legible human face. These are always split into separate shots
(A = wide hull shot, B = medium cockpit/character shot).
```

## 🎥 CAMERA BIBLE

```
LENS LOCK — exactly one lens per shot, never mixed:
- WIDE / ESTABLISHING / SHIP / LANDSCAPE-SCALE shots → 32mm Cooke anamorphic
- MEDIUM / EMOTIONAL / CHARACTER shots → 75mm
- EXTREME MACRO (tidal-stretch demonstration inserts) → 100mm macro

DISTANCE LOCK: every prompt states camera distance in meters explicitly.

MOVEMENT VOCABULARY (single continuous move per shot): push-in, pull-back, tilt-up,
tilt-down, slow orbit, fixed lockoff.

FRAMING RULE: no contradictory framing in one prompt (never "show the entire ship"
and "push toward her face" together — see Shot-Split Rule).
```

---

## 💡 LIGHTING BIBLE

```
PRIMARY SOURCES (named explicitly, never just "cinematic lighting"):
- harsh white-gold accretion-disk key light (the planet's dominant "sun," always
  from the tidal-axis direction)
- pale, cool secondary sunlight (the true distant star, softer and bluer, casting a
  second, fainter shadow)
- warm amber engine and suit-seam glow (ship thrusters, suit joint lighting)
- deep violet-black shadow in the terminator zones between stretch and compression
- soft golden rim-light from the photon ring silhouette when Karanoks is in frame

SHADOW BEHAVIOR (unique to this film): shadows cast on the substellar-facing terrain
are subtly, visibly elongated beyond normal optical expectation — a deliberate,
consistent visual signature of the tidal field, always oriented toward Karanoks.

COLOR GRADE (LUT LANGUAGE): harsh white-gold key light / pale cool secondary shadow /
deep violet-black terminator darkness / warm amber engine glow / soft photon-ring bloom.
```

## 🏃 MOTION BIBLE (Micro-Behavior Catalog)

```
Every shot includes at least one of the following, drawn from this fixed catalog:
- a slight, continuous forward-leaning brace in her stance, more pronounced facing
  the tidal axis
- brief pause and weight-shift before changing direction (adjusting to differential
  pull)
- fine dust drifting slowly and consistently toward the substellar horizon, never
  swirling randomly
- her own shadow visibly, subtly longer and thinner than it should be when facing
  Karanoks
- a slow, deliberate exhale, visible as faint condensation against her visor in the
  cooler air
- fingers flexing slightly, as if testing the faint pull on her limbs
```

## ⚙️ PHYSICS BIBLE

```
- Differential gravity: strongest at the substellar point, oriented always toward
  Karanoks. Feet experience measurably more pull than head when facing that axis.
- Dropped or dangling objects on the substellar-facing side fall and hang in
  fractionally elongated, thinned arcs/lines compared to Earth-normal — always this
  same specific visual signature, never randomized.
- Shadows and reflections elongate along the same tidal axis, consistently, scene to
  scene.
- Wind and drifting dust always move gently toward the substellar horizon — never
  swirl, never blow away from it.
- The ocean (Scenes 5-6) maintains a constant, visible slope toward the substellar
  horizon rather than a flat waterline.
- Human-to-environment scale is always internally consistent: Derin is 1.66m in
  every frame; the ship is 420m; landing struts are 16m; cockpit window is 2m.
```

---

## 🚫 NEGATIVE BIBLE (appended to every prompt)

```
No cuts. No shaky camera. Not miniature. Not toy-like. No CGI look. No airbrushed or
plastic skin. No perfect symmetry. No oversaturated colors. No random/chaotic dust
motion (must always drift toward the substellar horizon). No violent storm imagery
(tidal stretching is gentle and constant, not catastrophic). No handheld tools in
her hands. No text. No watermark.
```
