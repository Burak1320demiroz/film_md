## 🌍 WORLD BIBLE — GEZEGEN: ELYSION VE "THE SHROUD"

```
PLANETARY SURFACE (deliberate contrast): Elysion is a genuine garden world — soft,
Earth-like meadows of pale green and gold grass-analog vegetation, gently rolling
hills, a clear, slow-moving brook, scattered clusters of low, flowering plants in
warm coral and lavender hues. Standard 1.0g, breathable-pressure atmosphere (suit
worn for long-duration survey protocol and unknown biosphere protection, not
because the air is hostile). Gentle wind. Ambient insect-and-birdlike sounds are
present at ground level — this world reads, deliberately and convincingly, as a
paradise, at least from the ground looking outward toward the horizon.

CRITICAL WORLD RULE — THE SHROUD: What should be open sky above Elysion is instead
occupied, at a fixed orbital altitude, by an almost continuous shell of ship hulls
— "The Shroud." Over uncounted millennia, an ancient, now-vanished spacefaring
civilization built, launched, decommissioned, and eventually simply abandoned an
almost incomprehensible number of vessels in stable orbit around Elysion. Orbital
drift, gentle micro-collisions, and eventual gravitational settling over eons have
packed these ships hull-to-hull into a dense, semi-continuous shell — not a debris
field, but something closer to a solid, cracked ceiling made entirely of derelict
spacecraft, ranging from 100-meter utility craft to five-kilometer capital
dreadnoughts, all locked together in silent, frozen formation.

LIGHT THROUGH THE SHROUD: The Shroud is not perfectly solid — gaps, torn sections,
and spaces between overlapping hulls allow shafts of true daylight (from Elysion's
own sun, positioned beyond the Shroud) to pierce through in dramatic, cathedral-
like beams, some narrow and sharp, some broad and diffuse, moving slowly across
the meadow as the Shroud's component ships very slowly shift and the planet
rotates beneath them. This is the film's central visual signature: the ground
looks like paradise, lit not by an open sky but by light filtering through the
gaps in an ancient, drifting fleet.

FROM THE GROUND: Looking straight up, the visible "sky" during daylight resolves,
upon close attention, into overlapping hull-plates, dead running-light clusters,
docking-collar rings, and engine-nozzle silhouettes — an unmistakably artificial
ceiling once the eye adjusts, though it can initially, deliberately read as an
unusual but plausible cloud formation from a distance (used specifically for the
Scene 0 hook's misdirection).

THE THREE MOONS: Elysion's three moons were the ancient civilization's shipyards,
each converted to a distinct industrial function, still visible from the ground as
distinct, heavily-scarred discs in the rare open gaps of the Shroud:
- DÖKÜM ("The Forge"): covered in vast, empty dry-dock cradles, kilometers-long
  skeletal gantries still standing
- NEFES ("The Breath"): scarred with countless overlapping thruster-test craters
  and reactor-scorch rings
- KOMUTA ("Command"): smaller, smoother, still faintly broadcasting a slow,
  repeating automated beacon signal after millennia — the reason this expedition
  came at all

SOUND: Peaceful ambient ground-level life sounds (wind through grass, distant
water, insect-analog chirps) persist throughout, deliberately continuing even
during the film's most awe-inducing reveals — the paradise below never
acknowledges the graveyard above. A very faint, low, groaning resonance is
occasionally audible, felt more than heard, when a section of the Shroud shifts.
```

## 👤 CHARACTER BIBLE — DR. GÖKÇE BARANSEL

```
IDENTITY: 34-year-old Turkish orbital archaeologist and abandoned-fleet
cartographer. Drawn to Elysion after her deep-space listening array detected
Komuta moon's ancient, still-repeating beacon signal — the first sign of activity
from a supposedly dead system in recorded history.

FACE (photographed, not rendered): Warm, fair olive skin with realistic visible
pore texture and natural subsurface scattering under Elysion's dappled, shaft-lit
daylight — no airbrushing, no plastic smoothness. A soft, heart-shaped face with
high, delicate cheekbones. Thin, naturally arched light-brown eyebrows. Wide, pale
grey-green eyes with visible catchlight, faint natural fine lines at the corners
from years spent squinting at faint orbital telemetry. A small, slightly upturned
nose with a scatter of light freckles. Full lips, softly chapped. A thin, faded
scar along her left jawline from an old fieldwork accident. Subtle natural facial
asymmetry — a real, curious, quietly awe-struck human face.

HAIR: Light auburn, wavy, shoulder-length, pulled back loosely into a low,
imperfect ponytail, several realistic loose strands escaping near her temples and
forehead, tucked unevenly behind her ears where they meet the helmet seal.

BUILD: Slender, average height, 1.65m, a light, curious gait — she often walks
with her head tilted slightly upward, a habit from a career spent reading orbital
data rather than ground terrain.

SUIT: Pale cream and dusty-rose field survey suit, visibly mission-worn:
- fine pollen and grass-seed residue caught in every seam from days on the meadow
- a small, hand-embroidered patch of a stylized ship-silhouette sewn crookedly
  near her right shoulder — a personal touch, not standard issue
- scuffed boot soles from walking on soft, uneven ground
- a thin, cracked seal-strip along the collar, patched with silver tape
- only faint, aged pale-gold accent lines (echoing the sunlight filtering through
  the Shroud) rather than bright glowing lines
- a fully transparent, seamless helmet always revealing her complete face, with
  faint breath-condensation near the seal and a scatter of tiny seed-fluff caught
  against the outer glass
- HANDS ALWAYS EMPTY — no handheld tools, scanners, or antennae. All readings come
  from a single well-worn wrist display on her left forearm, its screen showing a
  live orbital-shell map and the beacon's repeating signal waveform.

MOVEMENT SIGNATURE: A habitual upward glance every few steps, checking the shifting
light-shaft patterns above almost unconsciously. Moves through the meadow with
soft, unhurried steps, occasionally crouching to let tall grass-analog brush
against her glove. A tendency to go completely still and silent when something in
the sky catches her attention.
```

## 🚀 SHIP BIBLE — SURVEY VESSEL _GÖZCÜ_ (THE WATCHER)

```
HULL LENGTH: 350 meters, stem to stern. Always anchored to this figure or a
visible reference object — deliberately modest in scale compared to the ancient
capital-ship hulls of the Shroud overhead, a scale contrast reinforced throughout.

DESIGN LOGIC: A long-range listening and archaeological survey vessel, its most
distinctive feature a large, dish-shaped signal array mounted dorsally,
constantly oriented toward Komuta moon's beacon.

STRUCTURE (in countable parts, never one smooth mass):
- a slender forward crew section, 110 meters, with a low, wide observation window
- a broad mid-section, 150 meters, dominated by the 45-meter dorsal listening
  dish, and rows of small observation ports, each approximately 1.2 meters in
  diameter, running its length as a scale ruler
- a compact aft engine section, 90 meters, housing two engine clusters, each 8
  meters in diameter
- four slender landing legs, 16 meters tall, ending in wide, flat 3-meter pads
  designed to rest gently on soft meadow ground without damage
- one forward observation window, 2.1 meters high by 4.2 meters wide

SCALE ANCHORS REQUIRED IN EVERY SHIP SHOT: at least one of — the 16m landing legs
against the meadow's grass-line, the 2.1m window with a human figure inside at
correct proportion, Gökçe's 1.65m figure against the hull, or — uniquely to this
film — the ship's modest 350m length directly compared against a single
kilometers-long derelict hull fragment visible overhead or crashed nearby, making
clear how small even a "large" human vessel is next to the ancient armada.

MANDATORY QUALIFIER IN EVERY SHIP PROMPT: "physically accurate perspective,
correct engineering proportions, realistic scale relationships — the ship must
never appear miniature, toy-like, or like a compact spacecraft."

SHOT-SPLIT RULE: A single frame never simultaneously demands (a) the full 350m
hull in view AND (b) a legible human face. Always split into separate shots (A =
wide hull shot, B = medium window/character shot).
```

## 🎥 CAMERA BIBLE

```
LENS LOCK — exactly one lens per shot, never mixed:
- WIDE / ESTABLISHING / SHIP / SKY-SHROUD shots → 32mm Cooke anamorphic
- MEDIUM / EMOTIONAL / CHARACTER shots → 75mm
- EXTREME MACRO (grass, hull-plate, beacon detail) → 100mm macro

DISTANCE LOCK: every prompt states camera distance in meters explicitly.

MOVEMENT VOCABULARY (single continuous move per shot): push-in, pull-back, tilt-up
(reserved as the signature move of this film, used for Scene 0's core reveal and
echoed in later scenes), tilt-down, slow orbit, fixed lockoff.

FRAMING RULE: no contradictory framing in one prompt (never "show the entire ship"
and "push toward her face" together — see Shot-Split Rule).
```

---

## 💡 LIGHTING BIBLE

```
PRIMARY SOURCES (named explicitly, never just "cinematic lighting"):
- warm, dappled golden-white sunlight, filtering through gaps in the Shroud in
  distinct, moving cathedral-like shafts — the film's dominant, ever-shifting
  light source
- soft, cool ambient shadow-light in areas where the Shroud is denser overhead
- warm amber suit and wrist-display glow
- deep charcoal-black silhouette shadow cast by overlapping hull-shapes on the
  ground wherever a shaft's edge falls
- faint, cold starlight bleed at the edges of larger Shroud gaps, visible even
  during "daylight," hinting at true space beyond

COLOR GRADE (LUT LANGUAGE): warm dappled golden-white shaft light / soft cool
ambient shroud-shadow / deep charcoal-black hull-silhouette shadow / warm amber
suit and console glow / faint cold starlight bleed at gap-edges.

SHAFT MOVEMENT RULE: light-shaft position and shape must shift subtly but
noticeably across any shot longer than a few seconds, reflecting both the
planet's rotation and the Shroud's own slow internal settling — never a static,
fixed lighting pattern.
```

---

## 🏃 MOTION BIBLE (Micro-Behavior Catalog)

```
Every shot includes at least one of the following, drawn from this fixed catalog:
- a habitual upward glance, checking the shifting light-shafts overhead
- going completely still and silent when something in the sky catches her
  attention
- fine grass-seed fluff or pollen drifting past her visor in the breeze
- fogging breath at the visor seal, clearing after a beat
- crouching to let tall grass brush against her glove
- fingers hovering near — not touching — an ancient surface or artifact
```

---

## ⚙️ PHYSICS BIBLE

```
- Standard gravity (1.0g) on Elysion's surface.
- The Shroud's light-shafts must always be shown as directional, moving, and
  gap-shaped (never a uniform "glow from the sky") — consistent with sunlight
  passing through irregular gaps between drifting hull-plates.
- Any Shroud movement (settling, shifting, breaking away) is extremely slow and
  silent from a distance, felt as a faint groan rather than heard as a loud crash
  — consistent with the World Bible's sound rule.
- Ground-level peaceful ambient details (grass motion, insect-analog sound, water
  flow) continue undisturbed even during major sky-level events — the contrast
  between paradise and armada must never break.
- Human-to-environment scale is always internally consistent: Gökçe is 1.65m; the
  ship is 350m; individual Shroud hull-fragments referenced range from 100m to
  5km, explicitly stated per scene.
```

---

## 🚫 NEGATIVE BIBLE (appended to every prompt)

```
No cuts. No shaky camera. Not miniature. Not toy-like. No CGI look. No airbrushed
or plastic skin. No perfect symmetry. No oversaturated colors. No uniform/flat sky-
glow (Shroud light must always read as shaft-shaped and gap-derived). No horror-
movie framing or hostile tone — the Shroud is awe-inspiring and melancholy, never
menacing. No handheld tools in her hands. No text. No watermark.
```
