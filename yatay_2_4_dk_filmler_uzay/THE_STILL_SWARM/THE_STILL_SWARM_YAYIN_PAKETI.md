### DESCRIPTION (§2) — film-specific lines only

```
Nothing in this debris field moves. Not even the things she throws into it.

A salvage operator goes twenty metres into a shell of held wreckage to reach a
power cell her shuttle needs. Inside, thrust does nothing and a tether does
nothing; the only way to move is to throw away what she carries, one piece at
a time.

THE SCIENCE
• With no thrust, changing your velocity in free fall means throwing mass the
  other way — the relation is Tsiolkovsky's rocket equation, published in 1903.
• Since 1994, NASA spacewalkers wear SAFER, a small nitrogen jetpack, for
  exactly one reason: a parted tether otherwise leaves no way back.
• Real orbital breakups do the opposite of this one — they spread. Donald
  Kessler described that cascade in 1978.
• In hard vacuum a vented plume is a straight, sharp-edged cone that thins
  quickly; there is no air to make it billow.

WATCH NEXT
```
