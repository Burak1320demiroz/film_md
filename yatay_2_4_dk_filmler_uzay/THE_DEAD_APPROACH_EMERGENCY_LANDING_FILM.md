## 🌍 WORLD BIBLE — AY: KORRATH (Cygnus-9f)

```
CLASSIFICATION: An unnamed, unmapped rocky moon (diameter: ~2,800 km,
slightly smaller than Earth's Moon) orbiting CYGNAR — a gas giant
approximately 8 times the mass of Jupiter, located in the outer Cygnus
arm of the Milky Way. KORRATH carries NO official designation in any
navigation database. It was detected as a gravitational anomaly during
a deep-range survey pass but never formally surveyed. This is why the
KESSLER has no surface data and why Commander Oron is landing blind.

CRITICAL WORLD RULE — THE TERMINATOR ZONE: The KESSLER makes its
emergency landing on KORRATH'S TERMINATOR LINE — the boundary between
the permanently sunlit hemisphere and the permanently dark hemisphere.
Because KORRATH is tidally locked to CYGNAR (the same face always
points toward the gas giant), the terminator is a permanent, fixed
geographic feature. At the landing site: the system's star (a distant
orange K-type) sits permanently at the horizon, exactly at the rim of
the largest visible crater. This creates PERMANENT ALACAKARANLIK
(twilight) — long, dramatic raking shadows across the regolith, the sun
never rising, never setting. Shadows are 10-15x longer than the objects
that cast them.

SURFACE: Ancient, heavily cratered basalt plains. The landing site
occupies a flat regolith plain approximately 8 km wide, bracketed by
two large crater rims — the NORTHERN RIM (200m tall, visible as a
dramatic dark ridge against the sky) and the SOUTHERN RIM (120m tall,
partially lit by the grazing sunlight). Between them: a flat expanse
of REGOLITH — fine gray-white mineral dust 25-40 cm deep covering
harder basalt bedrock. Scattered across the plain: angular impact
boulders, 0.5m to 6m in diameter, casting extremely long shadows.

REGOLITH PHYSICS: The dust is ultra-fine — similar to the Moon's
regolith, with the same static-charge clinging behavior. It sticks to
everything it contacts. In 0.12g, disturbed dust rises slowly in wide,
flat parabolic arcs and takes 3-5 times longer to settle than on Earth.
A footstep creates a puff that hangs in vacuum for 30+ seconds before
drifting back down. The KESSLER's landing raises a massive cloud that
disperses across kilometers.

CYGNAR IN THE SKY: The gas giant fills approximately 45 degrees of sky
as seen from KORRATH's surface — enormous, dominating the upper portion
of every exterior frame. CYGNAR has visible cloud banding:
- Dominant color: warm amber-ochre with cream-white high cloud streaks
- Polar regions: faint blue-grey
- A large oval storm system (darker amber) visible in the northern band
CYGNAR is the visual anchor of every exterior shot. Its reflected light
provides a warm amber-ochre ambient illumination on KORRATH's surface —
directional, from above and slightly left (since CYGNAR is not directly
overhead). This light is diffused and soft but directional — it creates
warm amber half-shadows on the far side of boulders and crater walls.

ATMOSPHERE: Effectively none. Trace gases, less than 0.001 atm —
absolute vacuum for all practical purposes. No sound propagates
outside the ship. No wind, no weather, no atmospheric scattering.
The sky is BLACK between CYGNAR and the stars — no blue, no haze.
Stars are sharp, non-twinkling points of light. The terminator-horizon
star is a bright orange point, never a disk.

GRAVITY: 0.12g. Approximately 70% lower than Earth's Moon. Footsteps
float — each stride has a brief moment of low-gravity suspension at
the top. Dust settles in slow, wide arcs. Loose objects fall at
1/8 Earth speed. Human movement requires careful foot placement —
too much force and a footstep can lift the body significantly.

TEMPERATURE: Surface at terminator: -110°C to -80°C. Not visually
represented (suit protects Yael) but creates visible frost patterns
on the KESSLER's hull in post-landing scenes — a thin layer of
condensate from the ship's own atmosphere, freezing on the exterior.

SOUND: TOTAL VACUUM outside the ship. Inside: cockpit ambience,
suit systems, VANCE's voice through speakers, hull stress groans
through structural transmission (felt, not heard — a deep vibration
sensed through the seat). On the surface, Yael hears only what
conducts through her suit: boot impact on regolith (low thud),
her own breathing (constant), VANCE's voice through helmet speakers.
```

## 👤 CHARACTER BIBLE — COMMANDER YAEL ORON

```
IDENTITY: 38-year-old Israeli-American deep-range scout pilot with 11
years of solo deep-range survey missions for the Cygnus Exploration
Authority. She is NOT a researcher — she is a pilot. She does not study
planets; she is the person who goes somewhere first, reports what she
sees, and gets out. KORRATH was a gravitational anomaly pass-by — a
routine sensor sweep that became catastrophic when an unmapped micro-
debris field shredded the KESSLER's left systems. She has never crashed.
She has never been this alone. She has been trained for this exact
scenario — and training feels nothing like the real thing.

LANGUAGE: English only. American accent (Israeli-inflected in stress
moments — harder consonants, clipped vowels). Short sentences. She
thinks in actions: not "what is happening?" but "what do I do next?"
When afraid, she does not go quieter like Elena — she goes FASTER,
decisions compressing. When exhausted (post-landing), she goes very
still, very quiet, as if rebooting.

FACE (photographed, not rendered): Warm medium-olive skin with visible
pore texture, natural subsurface scattering under every light condition.
A strong, square jaw and high defined cheekbones. Dark hazel eyes —
brown-green, intense, rarely blinking under pressure. Slight dark
circles from mission fatigue. A straight nose with a subtle bump at the
bridge — old break, healed long ago. Full lips, usually pressed into a
thin determined line when working. A small diagonal scar cutting through
her LEFT EYEBROW — a docking accident from year 2, healed but permanent.
Natural, asymmetric facial structure. No airbrushing, no perfect skin,
no artificial symmetry. Real face of someone who has lived in recycled
air for years.

HAIR: Dark brown, cut short — above the ears, the shortest hair of
any character in the series. Completely practical: no loose strands,
nothing that moves inside the helmet. Under the visor: a faint
perspiration sheen on the temples in crisis scenes (S1-S6).

BUILD: 1.70m, lean, athletic. A pilot's build — compact, efficient
movement. She does not waste energy on large gestures. Everything is
purposeful: reaching a switch is a single precise movement, not a
search. Under the adrenaline of S5 (landing), her shoulders tighten
visibly and her hands grip with knuckle tension.

SUIT — KESSLER SCOUT FLIGHT SUIT (Class-7 Scout Issue):
- base color: DEEP SLATE-GREY — a functional medium-dark grey with
  a faint blue undertone. Not black, not navy — specifically slate.
  Reads as almost-black in emergency red lighting, as medium grey
  in ambient exterior light.
- ELECTRIC BLUE accent stripes along: both shoulder caps (5cm wide),
  the collar ring (3cm band), and the outer calf panels (8cm wide,
  one per leg). These match the KESSLER's own hull color scheme —
  ship and pilot share the same color language.
- chest panel: three abstract status indicators in a triangular
  arrangement — left: slow blue oval pulse (O2 — steady = good),
  center: amber bar (suit pressure — full bar = nominal, dropping
  bar = warning), right: red circle indicator (communications —
  THIS IS DARK/OFFLINE for the entire film, as comms are severed).
  NEVER readable text, NEVER digits — abstract light shapes only.
- wrist display: compact, left forearm, showing TWO abstract readouts:
  a steady green pulse (suit integrity) and an amber numerical-looking
  pattern that is NEVER legible — abstract shifting amber segments
- HELMET: integrated into the suit collar, not a separate module.
  A single-piece visor housing in matte slate-grey with electric blue
  accent at the visor rim. FULL TRANSPARENT, SEAMLESS VISOR —
  always revealing her complete face. The visor housing has a small
  emergency release tab on the upper right (matte orange, 2cm tab).
- GLOVES: thin, articulated, slate-grey with blue-white silicone
  grip pads on the palm and fingertips. Worn — the grip pads are
  slightly compressed from years of use.
- BOOTS: standard magnetic scout boots, flat-soled, slate-grey with
  electric blue ankle LEDs — one on each ankle. In low gravity
  (surface scenes), the magnetic lock is partially engaged (amber
  LED) to prevent over-floating.
- HARNESS: emergency-grade bright orange webbing harness, visible
  across chest and shoulders in cockpit scenes. Tight during landing.
  Released in S7 — she unclips each buckle individually, one breath
  between each.

VISOR PERSISTENCE RULE [LESSON 5]: Visor ALWAYS VISIBLE in every
frame regardless of head angle. Face ALWAYS readable through visor.
No reflective coating, no tinting, no HUD graphics on visor.

SUIT CONTINUITY RULE [LESSON 8]: Every detail above remains IDENTICAL
across all scenes. Wear increases: perspiration sheen on temples appears
in S3, increases through S6, fades to exhaustion in S7-S10.
The orange harness is visible in S1-S7, removed/slack from S8 onward.

CONTACT INTEGRITY RULE [LESSON 11]: When touching ANY surface (flight
controls, console, helmet, suit clips, moon rock), hands/fingers
maintain COMPLETE STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY — clear visible boundary at
contact points. Gloves never merge with surfaces.

FACE LOCK [LESSON 16]: Identical facial structure — square jaw,
hazel eyes, eyebrow scar, nose bump — throughout ALL scenes.

MOVEMENT SIGNATURE: FAST AND PRECISE in crisis (S1-S5) — hands move
between controls without hesitation, head snaps between displays and
windshield. In S6 (impact): body absorbs the hit — braced, held tight.
Post-landing (S7-S8): deliberate, controlled exhaustion — each action
separated by a conscious breath. On surface (S9-S10): tentative in
the low gravity, placing each foot carefully but moving with growing
confidence. THE GRIP: her most visible stress tell — right hand wraps
around the throttle or armrest with visible white-knuckle tension.
```

## 🤖 AI VOICE BIBLE — VANCE (Ship AI)

```
IDENTITY: VANCE (Vehicle AI Navigation and Crew Emergency system) —
the KESSLER's onboard mission AI. Unlike SABLE (which has been Elena
Vasquez's companion for 8 years), VANCE is a mission-specific emergency
AI — newer, less personalized, built for crisis protocol delivery.
It is a VOICE ONLY — no physical avatar, no hologram. It speaks from
the cockpit's headrest speakers — close, behind the pilot's ears.

CRITICAL DIFFERENCE FROM SABLE: SABLE is a companion that happens to
manage systems. VANCE is a system that happens to speak. Where SABLE
nurtures and contextualizes, VANCE delivers data and moves on.

VOICE CHARACTER: Male-coded. Neutral, flat North American accent.
Rapid delivery — slightly faster than comfortable. Sounds like an
air traffic controller at maximum traffic load: efficient, factual,
zero emotional padding. It does not wait for Yael to process before
delivering the next data point. This creates the film's core tension:
a pilot who needs a moment, and an AI that doesn't give them.

LANGUAGE: English only. Clipped sentence structure. Subject-verb-object.
No qualifiers, no softening. "Left engine: offline." Not "I'm afraid
the left engine appears to have gone offline, Commander." VANCE strips
all redundancy.

DELIVERY PATTERN (3-beat, faster than SABLE):
1. DATA: "Left engine: offline."
2. CONSEQUENCE: "Thrust at 47%."
3. NEXT (sometimes omitted): "Manual compensation required."

USES "Commander" always. NEVER uses first name. Even in the film's most
desperate moment, VANCE says "Commander." This is not warmth — it is
protocol. Yael calling VANCE by name is the emotional signal, not the
reverse.

THE "TECHNICALLY" MOMENT: When Yael asks "Are we alive?" after landing,
VANCE says "Technically." This is not dry humor. It is a probability
assessment — they are technically alive given the damage sustained.
VANCE has no concept of what else "alive" might mean. This is the
film's single moment of dark comedy, delivered completely straight.

VANCE'S SYSTEM FAILURE VOICE: As power drops through the film, VANCE's
voice subtly degrades — not dramatically, but a faint compression
artifact, a very slight audio glitch between words. By S7, VANCE's
voice has a barely-perceptible digital edge that wasn't there in S1.

DIALOGUE RULES FOR VEO 3.1:
- All VANCE lines are VOICE-OVER — no character on screen speaks
  VANCE's lines. VANCE is a voice from headrest speakers.
- Prompt syntax: "a flat male AI voice speaks from the cockpit's
  headrest speakers, saying in a rapid clipped tone: '[line]'"
- When prompting Yael's dialogue: "the woman pilot says in a
  [controlled/tense/exhausted] voice: '[line]'"
- NEVER simultaneous speech. Turn-taking only.
- Each spoken line: under 5 seconds.
- Each scene: maximum 3 spoken lines total (1 or 2 exchanges).
```

## 🚀 SHIP BIBLE — DEEP-RANGE SCOUT VESSEL _KESSLER_

```
HULL LENGTH: 34 meters. A Class-7 Deep Range Scout Vessel — compact,
angular, built for solo long-range survey missions. Not a cargo vessel
(like ACHERON) and not a science lander (like DERINLIK) — a SCOUT.
It was designed to get somewhere fast, survey it, and get out. It is
faster and lighter than either.

DESIGN LOGIC: Angular, wedge-forward shape. The bow tapers to a point
(where the original navigation beacon array was mounted — now torn
off, leaving a ragged metal stump). The fuselage widens to a wider
midsection, then narrows again at the stern where the two main engine
pods mount on SHORT, FIXED pylons (non-articulating). The overall
silhouette: like a stretched arrowhead.

STRUCTURE (in countable parts):
- HULL: 34m long, DARK SLATE-GREY with ELECTRIC BLUE accent panels
  along the dorsal spine and leading wing edges — matching pilot's
  suit color scheme exactly (ship and pilot are visually coordinated)
- COCKPIT MODULE: angular enclosure at the forward end with a single
  WIDE CURVED WINDSHIELD (3m wide, 1.5m tall) — heavily scratched
  at the edges from years of micro-debris impacts, heat-stained at
  the lower corners. The windshield is the main visual portal for
  all approach and surface scenes.
- WINGS: two mid-mounted delta wings, 14m tip-to-tip. The LEFT WING
  TIP IS MISSING — torn off cleanly in the debris impact, leaving
  a jagged metal edge at approximately 70% of the wing length. The
  navigation beacon that was mounted there is gone.
- ENGINE PODS: two stern-mounted pods on fixed pylons. RIGHT ENGINE:
  active, producing blue-white exhaust (dimming through film as fuel
  depletes). LEFT ENGINE: completely offline — dark, no exhaust.
- SENSOR MASTS: three external sensor masts on the dorsal spine.
  The forward mast is bent at a 45-degree angle (impact damage).
  The middle and aft masts are intact but non-functional.
- LANDING LEGS: four articulated legs in landing configuration.
  LEFT FRONT LEG: collapses on contact in S6, causing the ship to
  tilt 8 degrees to port. The other three legs hold.
  LEFT FRONT pad: visible crushed against the regolith post-landing.
- UNDERBELLY: post-landing shows a 12m long scrape furrow from
  the initial contact point — polished silver where the hull paint
  was stripped, exposing bare metal beneath.
- LANDING LIGHTS: eight cool-white LED arrays on the underbelly,
  aimed downward. Active in S4-S6, illuminating the regolith surface
  in a cone of 5500K white light during approach.
- EMERGENCY BEACON: single amber-orange strobe on the dorsal spine,
  1-second cycle. Active from S0 onward — visible in all exterior
  shots as a rhythmic amber pulse.

COCKPIT INTERIOR (PRIMARY SET S1-S8):
- Single pilot seat, center-mounted, with BRIGHT ORANGE emergency
  harness webbing tight across chest and shoulders during S1-S6.
  The harness has four quick-release buckles — silver mechanical
  clips with orange pull tabs.
- The WINDSHIELD: KORRATH's surface and CYGNAR fill more and more
  of the view as the approach progresses.
- EMERGENCY LIGHTING: DEEP RED LED strips along the ceiling edge
  and floor seam — THE PRIMARY AMBIENT LIGHT for all cockpit scenes.
  After main power is diverted to engines (from S1 onward), only
  emergency red lighting remains. The cockpit is bathed in red.
- FLAT HOLOGRAPHIC DISPLAY PANELS (2): positioned left and right of
  the main windshield. Both show SYSTEM OFFLINE patterns — abstract
  red and amber flickering segments, NOT readable text. The left
  panel shows more red (system failures). The right panel shows a
  slow amber pulse (the single still-functional system: life support).
- MANUAL OVERRIDE PANEL: left armrest, physical toggle switches,
  two large red flip-covered switches (guards open, switches thrown),
  and several smaller toggles. The guards hang open — she's already
  activated every emergency override.
- THROTTLE: center console, right of the main stick. This is YAEL's
  primary contact point — the thing her right hand grips.
- PERSONAL ITEM: a worn fabric patch sewn onto the seat back behind
  her right shoulder — too small and dark to read, just a shape
  of worn color. Present in every cockpit shot.
- TILT: post-landing (S7-S8), the cockpit sits at 8-degree port
  tilt. Loose items in the cockpit have shifted slightly left. This
  tilt is PERSISTENT and IDENTICAL in every post-landing scene.

SHIP SCALE ANCHORS: Yael (1.70m) in the pilot seat. The windshield
(3m wide). The cockpit is INTIMATE — she can touch every switch
from the seat.

VEHICLE/STRUCTURE SCALE LOCK [LESSON 32]: All ship proportions —
cockpit window ratio, wing span relative to fuselage, landing leg
geometry (intact legs: 3m extended, 25-degree angle), engine pod
size — remain IDENTICAL across all scenes. The 8-degree port tilt
is EXACTLY 8 degrees in every post-landing frame where the ship
is visible.

DAMAGE PROGRESSION:
- S0 (Hook): left wing tip missing, left engine dark, sensor mast

- S1-S3 (cockpit, pre-landing): as above; interior emergency red only
- S4-S5 (approach): landing lights on, right engine bright and active
- S6 (landing): left front leg collapses, hull scrape, tilt locks
- S7-S10 (post-landing): all S6 damage plus: hull frost forming on
  exterior (ship atmosphere condensing on cold hull surface)

## 🎥 CAMERA BIBLE

```
LENS LOCK — exactly one lens per shot type, never mixed:
- WIDE / EXTERIOR / APPROACH / SURFACE shots → 32mm Cooke anamorphic
