### CUE A2 — "GÖLGE" (S0-S11 · ~2:10 üret, 1:58 kullan)

```
Instrumental only, no vocals, no lyrics. 120 BPM, 4/4, minor, driving
but controlled, bone-dry mix with no reverb at all — every sound dies
instantly, close and hard, as if played in thin dead air. A cold
countdown score for a man who has minutes, not hours. It opens with ONE
low sub-bass hit, alone; two seconds later a dry muted mechanical
tick ostinato opens up and runs continuously: a TWELVE-BEAT
cycle that repeats over and over, so that it never quite lines up with
the four-beat bar and keeps sliding against it. Under it a deep 45 Hz
sub-bass root doubled by a quiet companion tone one or two octaves
higher (roughly 90-160 Hz) so the weight survives on small phone
speakers; reinforce the low end with a dense harmonic stack at roughly
100 Hz, 150 Hz and 200 Hz so the missing fundamental is implied rather
than played; the perceived weight must survive full loss of everything
below 150 Hz. From there the piece ADDS one layer roughly every twenty
seconds, each entering exactly on a bar line and never leaving: a low
soft kick on half notes; a tight dry shaker-and-rim sixteenth grid; then
staccato low strings pumping a narrow two-chord ostinato locked hard to
the grid. It tightens rather than getting louder — no swells, no
crescendo, no fanfare, no heroic brass, no snare rolls, no choir, no
melody. Over the last ten seconds a mechanical riser builds using the
tick itself, sped up and layered, rising in pitch and density — NOT a
white-noise riser, no cymbal swell, no synthetic whoosh. The riser
reaches its top and the piece stops dead on a bar line with only the
sub-bass left ringing under it. No fade out. [Instrumental]
```

### CUE B2 — "ATEŞ" (S12-S19 · ~1:30 üret, 78 s kullan)

```
Instrumental only, no vocals, no lyrics. 120 BPM, 4/4, minor, hard
driving, bone-dry tight mix with no reverb — close, precise, sealed,
metallic. The launch and the run to the rendezvous. It opens on almost
nothing: a bare 45 Hz sub-bass root doubled by a quiet companion tone
one or two octaves higher (roughly 90-160 Hz) so the weight survives on
small phone speakers, reinforced by a dense harmonic stack at roughly
100 Hz, 150 Hz and 200 Hz so the missing fundamental is implied rather
than played, with the twelve-beat mechanical tick cycle sliding
underneath. It holds that emptiness for exactly three bars — tension,
no build. Then, ON A DOWNBEAT, everything lands at once: one enormous
low hit and full drive — pounding low toms, hard low string stabs, the
sixteenth grid at full tightness, the tick cycle still running through
it. This is the only big impact in the piece. It drives hard and evenly
for about twenty seconds without getting louder, then the ostinato
drops to HALF TIME — heavier, slower, wider, the same material at half
speed. Ten seconds later EVERYTHING STOPS for exactly one bar — total
silence, no tail — and returns with only the sub and the tick, quiet,
narrow and close, as if playing inside a helmet. From there it never
rebuilds: low strings hold ONE sustained chord that never resolves, the
grid thins out, the sub widens slowly, and over the last fifteen
seconds the layers strip away one by one until only the tick and the
sub are left, getting thinner. The piece ends by stopping dead on a bar
line — no fade out, no cadence, no final hit. [Instrumental]
```
