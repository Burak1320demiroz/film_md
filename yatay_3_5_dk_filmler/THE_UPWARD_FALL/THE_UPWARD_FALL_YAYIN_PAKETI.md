### DESCRIPTION (§2) — film-specific lines only

```
A forest where the dead go up, and something in the canopy is waiting for them.

A field biologist works alone under 400-metre trees, collecting one sample
from a canopy hunter he has never seen whole. The forest floor keeps letting
go of everything that has died on it. What he is carrying is the one thing
in his hands that wants to leave — and it is not the only thing that knows it.

THE SCIENCE
• Earth's trees stop growing near 122-130 m because sap cannot be pulled any
  higher without the water column breaking (Koch et al., Nature, 2004).
  Lower gravity moves that ceiling, which is why this forest can exist.
• Buoyancy in a thick atmosphere is ordinary physics, not anti-gravity: NASA
  Langley's HAVOC concept (2015) floats a crewed airship 50 km above Venus
  on nothing but breathable air.
• Titan, measured by Cassini-Huygens in 2005, runs at 1.45 atm and 0.14 g —
  the real example of what dense air plus low weight does to a body.
• Dead plant matter outgasses as it decomposes. On Earth that gas lifts
  vegetation off the floor of a bog. This film asks what a whole biosphere
  built on that would look like.

CHAPTERS
0:00 The first piece

1:59 The branch
2:59 The far light
```
