## 📝 DESCRIPTION

```
A regional dust front lands on a Mars outpost at last light. At six
hundredths of Earth pressure the wind cannot tip a cup — but the same dust
charges everything it touches, and at that pressure air breaks down at
roughly one hundredth of the field it needs on Earth. So the storm does not
push. It loads. And everything metal that is not bonded to ground becomes
somewhere for that load to go.

Two crew have three bonding lugs left to fix before the front arrives.
It arrives early.

25 shots. One locked-off camera each. No cuts.
```

## 🔬 VERIFIED SCIENCE

```
· Surface pressure on Mars averages about 610 pascals, roughly 0.6% of
  Earth's, and surface gravity is about 3.72 m/s² (0.379 g).
· Because dynamic pressure scales with air density, a Martian wind at a
  given speed carries a tiny fraction of the force the same speed carries
  on Earth — which is why Martian storms cannot topple structures or
  people, and why the storm in this film moves nothing.
· Wind-blown dust charges triboelectrically. Laboratory and modelling work
  on Martian dust devils and storms predicts significant electrostatic
  fields, and the low-pressure carbon dioxide atmosphere breaks down at a
  far lower field strength than air at sea level — so discharges occur at
  gaps that would be impossible on Earth.
· Grounding and bonding hardware exists for exactly this reason: a
  conductive path to ground is what stops accumulated charge from finding
  its own way through equipment.
· Sound at 610 Pa travels near 240 m/s and carries very little energy;
  high frequencies are lost almost entirely. A structural rupture is felt
  through the ground long before it is heard through the air.
· A pressure rupture into a 0.6%-pressure, 95% carbon dioxide atmosphere
  cannot sustain a flame: there is no oxidiser outside. What is seen is
  expanding interior gas, and it is over in under a second.
⚠️ HONEST NOTE: the exact discharge behaviour of a large Martian dust
  front has not been measured in situ. The pressure, gravity, sound and
  combustion figures above are established; the discharge scale in this
  film is an extrapolation from the low breakdown field, and the film
  keeps every discharge small, thin and brief for that reason.
```

## 📌 PINNED COMMENT

```
The wind in this film never moves anything — not a strap, not a cable
tail, not a boot. That is not a shortcut. At 0.6% of Earth's pressure a
Martian gale carries about as much force as a slow breath. Everything
that happens here happens because the storm is carrying charge instead.
```
