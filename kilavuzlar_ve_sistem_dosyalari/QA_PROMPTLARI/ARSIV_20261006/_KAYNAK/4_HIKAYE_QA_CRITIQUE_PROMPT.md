## WHAT THIS REVIEW IS NOT

Separate lenses cover the picture (FRAME), everything that must agree across a cut
(CONTINUITY) and the soundtrack (SOUND). **Do not report technical errors here.** A
film can be technically flawless and dramatically dead; your job is whether anyone
would care. Mention a technical issue only when it destroys a STORY beat — once.
```
❌ Physics, hidden cuts, bloom, lip-sync, particles, on-screen text, 9:16 framing,
   anything wrong INSIDE one frame                                → FRAME lens
❌ Counters, geography, time-of-day, cause/effect, props or costume that change
   between scenes, whether scene 12's person is scene 3's         → CONTINUITY lens
❌ Whose line it is, voice staying one voice, ambience, mix        → SOUND lens
   (whether the LINE IS WELL WRITTEN — subtext, exposition — is yours alone)
❌ The cover image (checked by hand) · in mode V anything you think is MISSING
```

## 🎯 THE SERIES' STORY DNA (judge against this, not against Hollywood)
2-9 minute solo sci-fi shorts for online distribution. INTENDED, NOT A FLAW: exactly
one human character · slow contemplative pacing, wonder over action · scientific
plausibility as pleasure · awe and existential calm, not horror · endings that hold
rather than resolve · silence over speeches · no villain (environmental, physical or
temporal force). ⛔ The list protects PRODUCTION CONSTRAINTS, not the film from the
question of stakes. STILL ASK, and report the answer even when all four are "no":
```
· one human            → does anyone want something from anyone — on screen, off screen,
                         or from a person who is gone? If nobody, that is a finding.
· no villain           → does the opposing force WANT anything from the protagonist? An
                         obstacle that wants nothing produces tension, not drama.
· endings that hold    → does it END, or does it STOP? Holding is a decision; stopping is
                         running out.
· silence over speech  → is anything WITHHELD? Silence with nobody to speak to is absence.
```
STILL A FLAW: no question in the first 10 s · scenes that repeat what was shown · a
camera-carrier instead of a person · a theme announced in dialogue · an ending that
stops · a premise a viewer cannot restate in one sentence.

### 1. 🪝 HOOK & FIRST 10 SECONDS (Kanca)
- What exact question does the opening pose (one sentence)? Legible in 2 s to a
  stranger? A SITUATION or merely a PLACE? A visual that stops a scroll? Does the
  hook promise something the film delivers?
- DELAYED START: seconds before anything is at stake; > 15 is a finding.
- SECOND-SCENE ESCALATION: does scene 2 add one NEW piece of information that
  ENLARGES the question (scale, count, direction)? A restatement is a finding.
- STATIC OPEN: the first frame must sit inside an ongoing, unfinished motion.
- DİKEY — timing law, scored inside this category: `0-2 s curiosity (name the reason
  not to swipe) · 2-5 s clues · 5-7 s full reveal · 7-8 s one held image`; report which
  beat is late, early or missing.
- SCORE: __/10

### 2. 💡 PREMISE & CLARITY (Önerme ve Anlaşılırlık)
- After one viewing, can a viewer state the film in ONE sentence? Write it as a
  viewer would; compare with the LOGLINE. Any point of confusion about WHERE, WHO,
  WHAT — cite the scene. Is the world's central rule established before it matters?
- UNEARNED KNOWLEDGE: does the film assume something it never showed?
- ⭐ DID THE IDEA ARRIVE? In one sentence, as a first-time viewer, what rule does this
  world run on? (Mode D: compare with the plan's novum.) The single most important
  line in the report — a film whose novum does not arrive has failed regardless of
  the rest. □ NO NARRATION NEEDED — does it transmit VISUALLY?
- SCORE: __/10

### 3. 🏗️ STRUCTURE & PACING (Yapı ve Tempo)
- Map the beats: setup, turn, climax, close. Turn past the halfway mark of a
  4-minute film is usually too late. Is the runtime justified — better 30 s shorter?
- DEAD SCENES: every scene removable with no loss (a 25-scene film usually has 2-4).
- REDUNDANT PAIRS: two scenes delivering the same information or beat.
- RHYTHM: shot scale and intensity vary, or one register throughout (the most common
  failure of contemplative shorts)?
- ESCALATION: name the scene where escalation stalls · SCENE NECESSITY: what does each
  scene add that no other adds? ⚠️ Before proposing a CUT ask what the scene is FOR —
  a distribution beat (hook, shareable frame) is not redundant for not advancing plot.
- SCORE: __/10

### 4. 👤 CHARACTER & INTERIORITY (Karakter)
- Does the protagonist WANT something (in their own terms)? A fear or wound the film
  SHOWS? Do they make CHOICES, or do things happen to them — cite the choices. A
  moment where we see them think? Do they change — and if not, is that meaningful?
- CAMERA-CARRIER TEST: could a drone replace this character with no loss? Severe.
- COMPETENCE: a trained professional, or a screenwriter's idea of panic?
- SCORE: __/10

### 5. 🎭 THEME & MEANING (Tema)
- What is the film about underneath the plot (one sentence)? Compare with THEME.
- DRAMATIZED (a choice with a cost) or ANNOUNCED (a line explains it)? Does the
  ending pay the theme off, or end near it?
- THE COST TEST: what does the protagonist give up? Nothing → the theme is decorative.
- SCORE: __/10

### 6. 💬 DIALOGUE & VOICE (Diyalog)
- Count the lines; is each necessary? Subtext, or everyone says what they mean? A
  specific person or generic competent-astronaut voice? Which lines would you cut?
- NARRATING THE VISIBLE: any line describing what the image already shows — quote
  every instance (the most common writing failure in AI shorts).
- BEST LINE: quote it and say why it works; none found is a finding.
- ⛔ FULLY SILENT FILM → `N/A`, not a number (declared constraint; out of the mean).
  One spoken line → score normally.
- SCORE: __/10 (or N/A — silent film)

### 7. 📈 EMOTIONAL ARC (Duygusal Yay)
- Plot intensity across the runtime: peaks, troughs, the low point. An arc, or a flat
  line with decoration? Is the climax earned by built pressure?
- UP-BEFORE-DOWN: a moment of hope before the worst moment — without it the fall has
  no height · BREATHING ROOM: at least one quiet beat to absorb; cite it or its absence.
- SCORE: __/10

### 8. 🏁 ENDING (Final)
- Does the final image land, or does the film stop? Is the last shot the RIGHT last
  shot — if not, which scene should have ended it? Does it recontextualise anything?
  Is the intended ENDING TYPE achieved — an ambiguous ending chosen, not unfinished?
- THE FINAL IMAGE TEST: describe the last frame in one sentence; it should contain the theme.
- SCORE: __/10

### 9. 🧬 ORIGINALITY & DIFFERENTIATION (Özgünlük)
- Against LIBRARY CONTEXT: does it repeat a sibling's premise, structure or beat?
  Against known cinema (The Martian, Gravity, Interstellar, Moon, Arrival, Ad Astra,
  Sunshine): if it echoes one, does it EARN the comparison?
- THE ONE THING this film does that nothing else does — state it; if you cannot, that
  is the most important finding in the review.
- SWAPPABILITY: could the setting be another planet with no story change? Then the
  world is wallpaper.
- SCORE: __/10

### 10. 📉 RETENTION RISK (İzleyici Kaybı) — judge as an audience
- The top three timestamps where a viewer stops, and why. Any stretch > 20 s with no
  new information, image type or escalation. Does staying get rewarded — a payoff a
  halfway-leaver would regret?
- COVER/TITLE ALIGNMENT: what the thumbnail promises, delivered WHEN? (Arriving at
  3:40 of 4:09 = structural problem.)
- THE SHAREABLE MOMENT: one 3-5 s passage someone would clip; timestamp or "none".
- DİKEY — the drop ladder, scored inside this category: `□ 0-3 s what is on screen and
  does it hold · □ ~0:10 has anything changed · □ ~0:30 is the strongest image still
  ahead · □ LOOP does the last frame cut back to the first · □ SILENT does it read
  with sound OFF`.
- SCORE: __/10
