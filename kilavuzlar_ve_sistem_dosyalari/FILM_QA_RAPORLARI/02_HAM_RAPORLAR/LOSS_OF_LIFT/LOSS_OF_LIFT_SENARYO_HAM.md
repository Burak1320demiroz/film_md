## THE THREE THINGS THAT MOST HURT THIS FILM
1. S15/S16/S17 (2:20-2:50) — Redundant worry beats — The film spends 30 seconds
   watching the character sit and look at tubes before the alarm goes off,
   killing the pacing just before the climax.
2. S6/S7 (0:49-1:10) — Stalled escalation — The man exits onto the balloon, but
   we get 21 seconds of standing and looking around before he actually begins
   his task in S8.
3. S3 (0:19-0:29) — Dead scene — The man sighing in the spherical room provides
   no new information following S2 and delays the introduction of the
   all-important gauge.

## THE THREE THINGS WORTH PROTECTING
1. S23 (3:40) — The single line of dialogue — "Ağırlık bende kalsın" (Weight
   stays with me) is a masterclass in format constraint. It is subtextual,
   emotional, and does not narrate the visible.
2. S18 (2:50) — The gauge alarm — Having the central conflict tied entirely to a
   physical, analog brass dial grounds the sci-fi setting and makes the
   invisible threat (loss of lift/gas) tangible.
3. S25 (4:00) — The final image — The visual of the small pod dropping away
   against the clouds perfectly answers the protagonist's choice without needing
   to show his face again.

## THE ONE THING NOTHING ELSE DOES
It builds its entire tension around a purely physical, vertical constraint
(altitude/lift) rather than a failing engine or a monster. The setting is the plot.
