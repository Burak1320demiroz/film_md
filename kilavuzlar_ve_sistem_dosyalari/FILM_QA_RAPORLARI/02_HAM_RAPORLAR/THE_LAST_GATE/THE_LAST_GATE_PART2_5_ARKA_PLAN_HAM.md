## THE THREE THAT MATTER

1. **Distant Figure Anatomical Fusion (Scenes 3, 10, 13)**: Background human figures consistently merge limbs, helmets, and torsos when rendered in midground queues.
* *Why it matters*: Destroys spatial realism and pulls viewer focus away from action to body-horror glitches.
* *Cheapest fix*: Re-frame / tight crop to exclude background rows, or matte out distal figures with clean static background plates.

2. **Garbled Emergent Pseudo-Text (Scenes 6, 14, 18)**: High-contrast console buttons and plaques resolve into shifting, unreadable AI glyphs.
* *Why it matters*: Draws immediate attention as a synthetic generative artifact.
* *Cheapest fix*: Composite real text graphics or blur non-subject console labels in post.

3. **Parallax Inversion & Single-Plate Backgrounds (Scenes 7, 19)**: Distant planetary surfaces lock speed with near structures or slide faster than foreground modules.
* *Why it matters*: Breaks environmental scale and renders space scenes flat and unnatural.
* *Cheapest fix*: Separate station foreground from planet background and re-render planet layer with correct orbital camera parallax.

## REVIEWER NOTES

The subjects of every shot—primarily central astronauts, hands operating controls, or hero spacecraft—are generated with high detail and fidelity. However, applying an inverted inspection lens reveals widespread degradation in the background field. Midground human figures degrade severely into merged torsos, three-legged geometry, and sliding walk cycles. High-contrast text on background consoles consistently devolves into AI pseudo-glyphs. Many of these issues can be mitigated in post-production via tight cropping or 2D graphic replacements without full re-generation.
